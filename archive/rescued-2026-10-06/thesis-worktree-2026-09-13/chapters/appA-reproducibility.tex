\paragraph{Implementations and their checks.}
The recursions, the estimators and the baselines live in \texttt{research/},
and the properties the text asserts of them are tested rather than assumed:
that moment-projected message passing on a non-Gaussian chain returns the
analytic Gaussian answer on the covariance-matched model, that the E-step is
exact and its pairwise statistic conserves mass, that EM ascends the exact
evidence, that the quadrature is converged in the grid, and that the
non-Markov reference collapses onto plain belief propagation when its extra
coupling is switched off. Fisher's identity is checked against a brute-force
enumeration of the posterior that shares nothing with the recursion but the
kernel and the grid, so a common error cannot satisfy both sides. The
forward--backward recursion is implemented twice in the package and the two
implementations are pinned against each other, which is what makes the
agreement check of Chapter~\ref{ch:gaussian-bp} independent rather than
self-confirming.

\paragraph{Provenance of the frozen outputs.}
The closed-form and quadrature studies are deterministic and run on a laptop;
the estimator comparisons ran under SLURM from the scripts in \texttt{hpc/}.
The confirmatory comparison and the capacity study (\texttt{exp\_31},
\texttt{exp\_32}) were deployed by a script that refuses a dirty tree, ships a
\texttt{git archive} of the commit and records its digest, and the non-Markov
study (\texttt{exp\_21}) is a clean-deployment rerun whose Gaussian arm
reproduces the run it replaces on every quoted number. The headline
comparison (\texttt{exp\_07}) predates that script and is certified instead by
an audit recomputed on every regeneration: the intersection of each run's
recorded dirty-file list with its own import closure is empty, and the single
row where it was not, $\nseq = 8192$, is withdrawn. The validation and
diagnostic runs carry their resolved configuration and seeds but an empty
revision field, and the claims resting on them are stated as measured curves
and orderings rather than as certified numbers.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Both arms of the headline comparison select their training budget on a
disjoint validation bundle, which agrees with the test-set optimum in
$\netagree\%$ and $\emagree\%$ of cells, and the screen that fixed the
baseline configurations ran on development seeds disjoint from the
confirmatory ones. Rerunning the smallest training set at the budget of the
largest moves its pooled ratio by $\budgetdelta \pm \budgetdeltase$, in the
direction that makes the reported table conservative for the structured arm.
A resolution gate excludes $\ndropped$ of $\ncellstotal$ cells whose narrowest
fitted component is under two grid cells wide; including them changes no ratio
by more than $\dropshift$.

\paragraph{Environment.}
Python $\ge 3.10$ with NumPy, SciPy, pandas and Matplotlib, float64
throughout, and the neural baselines in pure NumPy with an explicit random
generator, so a run reproduces from its recorded seed on a fixed
installation; the dependency list is a set of floors rather than a lock, and
byte-level reproduction across installations is not claimed. The document
compiles in one run of \texttt{tectonic main.tex}, and \texttt{check.sh}
reports unresolved references, undefined citations, duplicate labels and
overfull boxes.

\paragraph{How the reported ratios were aggregated.}\label{sec:aggregation-headline}
A ratio is a nonlinear function of two means, so it depends on the order in
which the averaging is done, and both reported ratios were recomputed under
alternative aggregations of the same frozen cells, refitting nothing. The
headline estimand is the schedule-pooled paired per-seed ratio of
\eqref{eq:em-pooling}. Its direction survives every alternative: each paired
bootstrap interval over seeds sits well clear of one, no aggregation produces
a reversal at any training-set size, and EM--BP is beaten outright in
$\nreversed$ of $\ncellsused$ cells. For the structure-aware comparison the
reported estimand is not the largest of the four, and the floor across them is
$\structaggfloor\times$ with a spread of $\structaggspread$
(Table~\ref{tab:aggregation-structured}), so the reading of a low single-digit
multiple at the smallest training set growing to roughly six at the largest is
a property of the data rather than of the summary.
