\paragraph{The score is a posterior mean.}
Tweedie's identity gives $\score(\vx,t)=-(\vx-e^{-t}\pmean{\va})/\Dt$, with
$\pmean{\va}=\E[\va\mid\vx_t=\vx]$ \citep{robbins1956empirical,efron2011tweedie}. Estimating the
score of a Markov chain under \cref{eq:channel} is therefore the same problem as computing
a posterior mean, that is, as denoising.

\paragraph{The posterior is a tree.}
Conditioning \cref{eq:prior} on $\vx$ multiplies the prior by one unary factor
$\like(x_i\mid a_i)\propto\exp[-(x_i-e^{-t}a_i)^2/2\Dt]$ per site (\cref{fig:mechanism}): no
edge is added among the latent variables, the posterior factor graph is the prior's chain, and
sum-product on it is exact \citep{pearl1988probabilistic,kschischang2001factor}. Two sweeps of
the functional messages
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \lmsg_i(a)&\propto\!\int\!\kernel(a\mid a')\,\like(x_{i-1}\mid a')\,\lmsg_{i-1}(a')\dd a',\\
  \rmsg_i(a)&\propto\!\int\!\kernel(a''\mid a)\,\like(x_{i+1}\mid a'')\,\rmsg_{i+1}(a'')\dd a'',
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bp}
\end{equation}
with $\lmsg_1\equiv\mu$ and $\rmsg_{\nsites}\equiv1$, return the site marginals
$\propto\lmsg_i\like\rmsg_i$ and the pair marginals $\propto\lmsg_i\like\,\kernel\,\like\rmsg_{i+1}$
of the whole sequence. Their means give the exact score of a correlated, possibly strongly
non-Gaussian, $\nsites$-dimensional law, for \emph{any} kernel, even one whose score has no closed
form. The pass is a smoother, not a filter: every site is conditioned on the whole sequence. It
needs the kernel only through the integral operator in \cref{eq:bp}, which is why everything
that follows treats $\kernel$ as the single object to be known, parameterised or learned.

\paragraph{What ``exact'' means here.}
Messages are functions on $\Reals$. We carry them on a grid of $\ngrid$ points on
$[-\halfwidth,\halfwidth]$ with composite trapezoidal weights, so three statements are kept
apart (\cref{app:numerics}): sum-product is exact on functional messages; forward--backward is exact
for the discretised factor model, whose evidence is exact to floating point; and that model
approximates the continuous chain through truncation (mass beyond $\pm\halfwidth$) and quadrature
(second order in the spacing), both of which we measure rather than assume. At $\ngrid=401$ points
on $[-8,8]$, the operating grid of every experiment below, the reference is accurate to
$10^{-5}$ relative on ordinary draws for every driving-noise family of
\cref{eq:linear}; on a Gaussian chain, where two independent exact answers exist, it is accurate to
$\gsOpWorst$ (\cref{sec:sanity}); and on the $\phi^4$ field it agrees with a grid four times finer to
$\fldOracleGridWorst$ on every cell (\cref{sec:field}). The domain, not the spacing, is what a
heavy-tailed family tests, and at $\corr=0$ the Student-$t$ family needs a wider one
(\cref{app:numerics}). Throughout, ``the exact score'' means this tree-exact inference, evaluated by
validated quadrature, and ``the oracle'' is BP with the true kernel. The cost is $O(\nsites\ngrid^2)$
per sequence and level, and one sweep also returns the pair marginals that EM needs
(\cref{sec:learning}); \cref{sec:compute} measures it.

\paragraph{Sampling.}
The same recursion samples. Drawing $a_1\sim\mu$ and then each $a_i$ from $\kernel(\cdot\mid a_{i-1})$
on the grid is exact ancestral sampling from the chain, and a forward-filter backward-sample pass
draws whole sequences from the posterior given $\vx$, which gives uncertainty as well as a mean
(\cref{app:field}, on the $\phi^4$ field). On a chain the reverse process is therefore needed to \emph{learn} the
kernel from noised data, not to sample from it once it is known (\cref{sec:experiments}).
