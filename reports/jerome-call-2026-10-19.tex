\section{The model: why a snapshot is a Markov chain}
\label{sec:model}

The one-dimensional stochastic Allen--Cahn equation (we write $\Temp$ for the temperature),
\[
\partial_\tau\phi=\partial_x^2\phi-V'(\phi)+\sqrt{2\Temp}\,\eta(x,\tau),\qquad V(\phi)=\tfrac14(\phi^2-1)^2,
\]
with $\eta$ space--time white noise, is the gradient flow of $H[\phi]=\int\big[\tfrac12(\partial_x\phi)^2+V(\phi)\big]\dd x$ shaken
at temperature $\Temp$. Its stationary law is therefore the Gibbs measure $p[\phi]\propto e^{-H[\phi]/\Temp}$: domains near
$\pm1$ separated by kinks, long when cold (Figure~\ref{fig:chain}c).

\textbf{Markov in space, exactly.} On a lattice of spacing $\latt$ the energy couples only neighbours,
$H_\latt=\sum_i\big[(\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i)^2/2\latt+\latt V(\phi_i)\big]$, so
\[
p(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_\nsites)\;\propto\;\prod_i\exp\Big(-\frac{(\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i)^2}{2\Temp\latt}-\frac{\latt\,V(\phi_i)}{\Temp}\Big),
\]
a product of nearest-neighbour factors: a snapshot is a Markov chain in the site index, with no approximation. Its kernel
comes from the transfer operator $\transfer(\phi,\phi')=e^{-\latt V(\phi)/2\Temp}e^{-(\phi'-\phi)^2/2\Temp\latt}e^{-\latt V(\phi')/2\Temp}$
and its Perron pair $\transfer\psi_0=\lambda_0\psi_0$:
\[
\kernel(\phi'\mid\phi)=\frac{\transfer(\phi,\phi')\,\psi_0(\phi')}{\lambda_0\,\psi_0(\phi)}
=\frac{\normal{\phi';\phi}{\Temp\latt}\,g(\phi')}{Z(\phi)},\qquad g=\psi_0\,e^{-\latt V/2\Temp},\qquad \pi=\psi_0^2 ,
\]
a Gaussian step re-weighted at the arrival point (a ``tilted walk''). So BP is the transfer-matrix computation and the exact
score is available (grid BP, exact to $\fldOracleGridWorst$). The drift is non-linear, $\E[a'-a\mid a]=\bondvar(\log Z)'(a)$ on
the standardised field $a=\phi/s$ with step variance $\bondvar=\Temp\latt/s^2$, so the paper's kernel $a'=\corr a+\varepsilon$
(linear drift) is misspecified. The correlation length $\corrlen=1/\log(\lambda_0/\lambda_1)$ grows like $1/\latt$ under refinement
and like $e^{E_{\mathrm{kink}}/\Temp}$ as the field cools; there is no transition in one dimension.

\section{The estimator: your pair energy, learned through the noise}
\label{sec:estimator}

\textbf{Model.} A Gaussian bond plus a site energy, five parameters, with the truth in the family ($\bondvar=\Temp\latt/s^2$,
$f=(\latt/2\Temp)V(sa)+$const):
\[
W_\params(a,a')=\frac{(a'-a)^2}{2\bondvar}+f_\params(a)+f_\params(a'),\qquad f_\params(a)=\sum_{m=1}^{4}c_m\Big(\frac a2\Big)^m.
\]
We call this arm the \emph{site energy}. Its kernel is the ground-state transform of
$e^{-f_\params(a)}\normal{a';a}{\bondvar}e^{-f_\params(a')}$, and a fit reads as physics:
$\hat\Temp=\hat\bondvar s^2/\latt$, $\hat V(\phi)=(2\hat\Temp/\latt)\hat f(\phi/s)+$const.

\textbf{Learning.} From noisy data only, $\vx=e^{-t}\va+\sqrt{\Dt}\,\vz$. Fisher's identity makes EM your Boltzmann learning with
a posterior in place of the data:
\[
\nabla_\params\log p_\params(\vx)=-\,\E_{p_\params(\va\mid\vx)}\Big[\textstyle\sum_i\nabla_\params W_\params(a_i,a_{i+1})\Big]
+\E_{p_\params(\va)}\Big[\textstyle\sum_i\nabla_\params W_\params(a_i,a_{i+1})\Big].
\]
The E-step is exact BP on each noisy sequence; the M-step refits $\params$ to the expected pair counts. The score then follows by
Tweedie, $\nabla_\vx\log\Pt(\vx)=-(\vx-e^{-t}\E[\va\mid\vx])/\Dt$, with $\E[\va\mid\vx]$ from BP with the learned kernel.

\section{The experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\textbf{Six cells.} A temperature sweep $\Temp\in\{0.3,0.5,1,2\}$ with $\latt$ solved to hold $\corrlen\approx6$ sites, and a
refinement $\latt\in\{1,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\}$ of the base cell $\Temp=0.3$ at fixed physical length ($\nsites=32,64,128$).
\textbf{Arms.} EM--BP with five kernels: site energy (5 numbers), spline tilt (25), Legendre tilt (7), the paper's mixture (25),
an MDN (1{,}516); a window CNN and a transformer, trained on one noisy copy per sequence (ISM) or on clean targets (DSM, more
information); the Gaussian closure, i.e.\ the best linear estimator (``linear floor''); the exact oracle.
\textbf{Protocol.} $\nseq\in\{32,128,512,2048\}$ sequences, sixteen seeds, predictions registered before each run.
\textbf{Metric.} $R=\big(\sum\|\hat\score-\score\|^2/\sum\|\score\|^2\big)^{1/2}$ over twelve noise levels $t\in[0.05,3]$, reported
as $R$ divided by the linear floor: $1$ is the Gaussian closure.

\item \textbf{The five-parameter site energy is best on every cell} (Table~\ref{tab:fieldscore2048}). At $\nseq=2048$ its error is

\item \textbf{The kernel family is the modelling choice.} The paper's mixture learns nothing beyond the Gaussian closure
($\fldMixFloorMin$--$\fldMixFloorMax$ times it): a linear drift cannot hold a domain.
\item \textbf{The fit is the physics} (Figure~\ref{fig:potential}): $\hat\Temp$ within $10\%$ in $\fldThetaOkFits$ of
$\fldThetaFits$ fits, barrier within $20\%$ in $\fldBarrierOkFits$ of $\fldBarrierFits$; samples drawn from it match the exact
kink density within $\fldKinkDevSite$.
\item \textbf{Your refinement prediction holds.} From $\latt=1$ to $\tfrac14$ the window CNN goes from $\fldWindowFloorHone$ to
$\fldWindowFloorHzeroptwofive$ times the floor (radius re-chosen per lattice); the site energy stays at $3$--$4\%$.
\item \textbf{Speciation} (Figure~\ref{fig:speciation}): colder fields commit earlier ($t_c$ from $\fldSpecTcCold$ at $\Temp=0.2$
to $\fldSpecTcHot$ at $\Temp=2$), and earlier than their Gaussian twin (by $\fldSpecExcessCold$ to $\fldSpecExcessHot$ in $t$).
\item \textbf{Caveats} (backup, B7). BP is slower per sequence than a network ($\cmpRatioCpu\times$ the transformer,
eight threads, 401 points; 101 points lose nothing here and make BP $\cmpCoarseSpeedupHundred\times$ faster than on 401). EM and
the DSM networks stop on the exact score of a validation set (a likelihood rule for EM gives $4$--$23\%$); the ISM networks do not.
A neural kernel wins only off-class (backup, B6).
\end{enumerate}
