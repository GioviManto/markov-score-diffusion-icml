\section{The question and the laboratory}

A diffusion model learns the score of a noised data law, $\nabla_x\log P_t(x)$, and for real data
nobody knows the target. We built a family where it is known exactly and is not trivial: a
first-order Markov chain of length $L$ with non-Gaussian innovations (Laplace, Student-$t$,
uniform, bimodal, a one-parameter family in between), noised coordinate by coordinate. Because
the noise attaches one factor per site, the posterior stays a chain and two sweeps of exact
message passing return the score. The covariance of every innovation family is the same by
construction, so the laboratory separates second-order structure (correlation $\rho$) from
higher-order structure (the shape of the innovation law) cleanly. \comp{Chapters 4, 7, 11}

Three questions were asked of it. What information survives the noise, order by order? Can the
dynamics be learned from noised sequences alone, and how efficiently? And what does an
estimator that knows the structure buy against a neural denoiser that does not, and where does
that stop?

\section{What stands, in one line each}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \hi{Noise is a low-pass filter on statistical order.} The Hermite moment of degree $m$ is
attenuated exactly by $e^{-mt}$; the Fisher information about a feature first visible at degree
$m$ decays as $e^{-2mt}$; the number of sequences needed for a fixed local precision grows as
$e^{2mt}$. Correlation lives at degree two, shape at degree four. Verified on the exact score,
with the constants. \comp{Chapters 3, 5, 10}

\item \hi{Second-order closure has an exact price.} Gaussian message closure computes the LMMSE of
the covariance twin (Proposition~1), and the gap to the true score is
$\tfrac16\|\kappa_4\|_F^2e^{-8t}$ to leading order. Adding the fourth-order term back closes
$\audEdgeClosedLaplaceTzeropsixeight$ of the gap at $t=\audEdgeLevelTzeropsixeight$ and all of it
from $t=2$. \comp{Chapters 10, 13}

\item \hi{The dynamics are learnable through the noise, and the information curve says where
learning stops.} From $\learnSize$ sequences the shape is recovered at the reference up to
$t=\learnLevelTzeropfour$; fits split at $t=\learnLevelTzeropseven$
($\audCollapsedTzeropseven$ of $\audCollapsedNTzeropseven$ collapse to a Gaussian innovation) and
collapse at $t=1$, one level below the point $t=\learnKurtBoundCross$ where the Cram\'er--Rao
reference crosses the coordinate's range. The correlation survives to the crossover.
\comp{Chapters 5, 15}

\item \hi{Capacity buys exactly the fourth-order structure.} A Gaussian-innovation kernel lands on
the LMMSE floor ($\capEmoneD$ against $\lmmseFloorLaplace$); an eight-component mixture reaches
$\capEmeightD$ at $M=2048$. Across the generalised-Gaussian family the gain is ordered by
$\kappa_4$ and reverses at the Gaussian member. The contrast holds in all $\regCells$
$(\rho,L)$ cells of a regime sweep at $\regSeeds$ seeds, the paired difference running from
$\regGainStrongest$ to $\regGainWeakest$ with every interval excluding zero, and in every cell the
$C=1$ arm lands on the LMMSE floor to $\regIdentMax$, which checks the whole apparatus.
\comp{Chapters 13, 15, 17}

\item \hi{Against networks, the advantage is largest at equal information.} Under the standard
recipe (clean sequences, fresh corruption every step) the best of six architectures reaches
$\baseBestD$ at $M=2048$, a paired factor $\baseRatioBestD$ behind EM--BP. At EM--BP's own
interface (one noised view per sequence, no clean chain, no true score for model selection) the
best network reaches $\matchedBestD$, a paired factor $\matchedRatioBestD$
$[\matchedRatioBestDlo,\matchedRatioBestDhi]$; given the clean sequence as a target but still one
corruption, $\matchedFixedD$, a factor $\matchedFixedRatioD$. \comp{Chapters 8, 17, 19}

\item \hi{That comparison now holds across regimes, and it is not about heavy tails.} Repeated at
$\rbCells$ combinations of correlation and chain length, with the networks re-screened inside each
regime and $\rbSeeds$ seeds per cell, EM--BP is ahead in every cell and in every seed, by paired
factors from $\rbIsmRatioEvfiveonetwentyeight$ to $\rbIsmRatioEvninefivethirtytwo$. Repeated at the
frozen point with a platykurtic innovation instead of a heavy-tailed one, the factor is
$\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwo$
$[\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwolo,\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwohi]$, the same as
on the Laplace chain. What the structure buys is fourth-order structure of either sign.
\comp{Chapters 17, 19}

\item \hi{The advantage ends where the structure is wrong, at a strength we can compute.} Under an
AR(2) coefficient or a site-dependent $\rho$ the ordering reverses inside the swept range; a
rank-one global latent or a long-range coupling never reverses it. The large-sample error of the
misspecified EM--BP is a closed-form Kullback--Leibler projection bias $b(\varepsilon)$ that
reproduces the measured errors to within $\audBiasMaxAbsDevLagoneD$. \comp{Chapter 18}
\end{enumerate}

\section{What survives the noise, order by order}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_theory.pdf}
\caption{\emph{(a)} Exact Fisher information about the correlation in the Gaussian chain,
divided by its predicted asymptote $\tfrac12\|\partial_\corr\Sigma_0\|_F^2e^{-4t}$: every curve
reaches $1$, and the crossover $\tstar=\tfrac12\log\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_0)$ (ticks) says when.
\emph{(b)} Information per sequence about the correlation (degree two) and the innovation shape
(degree four) of the generalised-Gaussian chain, against the guides $e^{-4t}$ and $e^{-8t}$.
\emph{(c)} The price of second-order closure, $\mathbb{E}\|S_t-S_t^G\|^2$ measured by exact message
passing and divided by the predicted $\tfrac16\|\kappa_4\|_F^2e^{-8t}$: the families with finite
eighth moments converge to $1$.}
\end{figure}

\noindent
This is the spine of the paper and it is computation, not fitting. Under the
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck channel the Hermite moment of degree $m$ is attenuated exactly by $e^{-mt}$,
so the Fisher information about a feature that first appears at degree $m$ decays as $e^{-2mt}$
and the sequences needed for a fixed precision grow as $e^{2mt}$. Correlation sits at degree two
and shape at degree four, which is why they are lost at different speeds and why the gap between
them widens with $t$. Panel (c) is the sharpest version: the entire cost of approximating a
non-Gaussian chain by its covariance twin is $\tfrac16\|\kappa_4\|_F^2e^{-8t}$, and adding the
fourth-order term back closes $\audEdgeClosedLaplaceTzeropsixeight$ of it at
$t=\audEdgeLevelTzeropsixeight$ and all of it from $t=2$.
\comp{Chapters 3, 5, 10, 13}

\section{What capacity buys}

\noindent
A Gaussian-innovation kernel ($\ncomp=1$) cannot beat the LMMSE of the covariance twin, and does
not: it sits on that floor at every sample size. The mixture goes far below it, and the margin is
the fourth-order structure and nothing else --- across the generalised-Gaussian family the gain is
ordered by $\kappa_4$ and vanishes exactly at the Gaussian member.

The contrast is not a property of the frozen corner. Repeating it over five correlations and three
chain lengths at $\regSeeds$ seeds gives the same answer in all $\regCells$ cells, with every
interval excluding zero:

\noindent
Two things are worth taking from that table beyond the result. The margin shrinks as $\corr$ rises,
in the direction the theory gives: a strongly correlated chain has a nearly Gaussian marginal
(excess kurtosis $3(1-\corr^2)/(1+\corr^2)$), so there is less fourth-order structure
to buy. And the $\ncomp=1$ arm lands on the LMMSE floor in every one of the $\regCells$ cells,
which tests the grid, the message passing, the EM and the scoring simultaneously, at fifteen
independent configurations. The sixteen-seed run also reproduces its eight-seed predecessor
exactly: $\regReproRows$ shared rows recomputed in a different directory, from a different
deployment, on a different node, agree to $\regReproMaxDiff$.

\smallskip
\noindent
One correction belongs with this result rather than in a footnote. The capacity sweep in the
archival record --- the one Chapter~10 of the thesis rests on, $\ncomp\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$ at
$\nseq\in\{128,512\}$ over sixteen seeds --- is void, for a reason prior to any reading of it:
its training draw re-created the random generator for every sequence, so every arm at every
capacity and seed was fitted to a single chain of $\nsites$ sites, its validation and test
bundles alone drawn correctly. Its own numbers carry the signature: a correctly fitted
$\ncomp=1$ kernel must sit on the LMMSE floor $\lmmseFloorLaplace$, which every cell of the
table above reaches to $\regIdentMax$, and the old sweep's single-Gaussian arm scatters across a
range several times wider. The earlier diagnosis --- that a stopping rule explained the
conclusion --- is withdrawn with it; the asymmetry is real but is \textsc{em}'s behaviour on one
chain. Nothing in this section depends on that sweep: the runs behind it draw every chain
independently and assert that they did.
\comp{Chapters 13, 15, 17}

\section{Structure against networks, at equal information}

This is the claim a referee will press hardest, so it is worth being precise about what is being
compared. Both sides see exactly one noised view of each sequence, drawn once. Neither side ever
sees the clean chain. Neither side uses the true score to choose when to stop: the networks select
on the implicit objective of a held-out noisy bundle, EM--BP on the held-out one-view evidence.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\caption{Interface~C across correlation and length (\textsc{e14}, $\rbSeeds$ seeds, $\nseq=2048$,
networks re-screened inside each regime). Columns: the \textsc{lmmse} oracle; EM--BP with its
iteration count chosen by held-out evidence (ev.) and by validation against the true kernel (bp);
the best network trained by implicit score matching on the same one-view bundle (C); the best
network given the clean sequence with one fixed corruption (B); and the paired factor of the C
network over the evidence-selected EM--BP, with a percentile bootstrap over seeds.}
\input{../paper/sections/regime-baselines-table}
\end{table}

\noindent
EM--BP is ahead in every one of the $\rbCells$ cells and in every one of the $\rbSeeds$ seeds of
each, by factors from $\rbIsmRatioEvfiveonetwentyeight$ to $\rbIsmRatioEvninefivethirtytwo$. The
factor is also conservative: the evidence-selected EM--BP is still improving when its iteration
ladder runs out in \rbEmEvAtCapTotal{} of the cells, so its error there is an upper bound on what
a longer ladder would give.

Three readings of the table matter more than the headline number.

\begin{itemize}
\item \hi{The advantage is smallest where the chain carries the most higher-order structure.}
At $\corr=0.5$ the marginal's excess kurtosis is $\rbKurtfivethirtytwo$ and the factor is
$\rbIsmRatioEvfivethirtytwo$; at $\corr=0.95$ the kurtosis is $\rbKurtninefivethirtytwo$ and the
factor is larger. Above $\corr=0.85$ the two chain lengths part company, so we report the direction
and not a law.

\item \hi{Length does not rescue the network.} At $\nsites=128$ each noised view carries four times
as many sites, and both arms improve --- the network from $\rbIsmBesteightfivethirtytwo$ to
$\rbIsmBesteightfiveonetwentyeight$ at $\corr=0.85$, EM--BP likewise --- so the factor survives
rather than being an artefact of short chains.

\item \hi{Where the network sits relative to the second-order floor is a statement about the
floor.} The network's error changes little across the sweep while the floor falls steeply with
$\corr$. So the network is below the floor at \rbIsmBelowFloorCells{} and above it at
\rbIsmAboveFloorCells{} --- at $\corr=0.95$, $\nsites=32$ by
$\rbIsmMinusFloorninefivethirtytwo$
$[\rbIsmMinusFloorninefivethirtytwolo,\rbIsmMinusFloorninefivethirtytwohi]$, against an exact
radius-$8$ floor of $\audLocFloorreightRhozeropninefive$ at that correlation. Where the covariance
carries most of the score energy, one view per sequence is not enough for a network to learn even
the covariance, while the estimator that knows the factorisation recovers it.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
\hi{And it is not about heavy tails.} Repeated at the frozen point with a \emph{platykurtic}
innovation --- a symmetric two-component mixture, kurtosis on the other side of Gaussian --- the
factor is $\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwo$
$[\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwolo,\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwohi]$ over
$\rbBimodalSeeds$ seeds, with the best network (\rbBimodalIsmBestArcheightfivethirtytwo) at
$\rbBimodalIsmBesteightfivethirtytwo$ against a floor of $\rbBimodalLmmseeightfivethirtytwo$ ---
$\rbBimodalIsmOverLmmseeightfivethirtytwo$ times above it. What explicit structure buys is
fourth-order structure of either sign.
\comp{Chapters 8, 17, 19}

\section{Where the advantage ends}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_misspec.pdf}
\caption{\emph{Top row:} ratio of a screened radius-2 window head's pooled error to EM--BP's
against the strength of four departures from the first-order Markov assumption, at two sample
sizes; above the dotted line the misspecified chain estimator still wins. \emph{Bottom row:}
EM--BP's own error at both sizes (points, paired standard errors) against the closed-form bias
$b(\varepsilon)$ of the Kullback--Leibler projection of the perturbed truth onto the chain family
(dotted) and the finite-sample prediction $\sqrt{b(\varepsilon)^2+v/\nseq}$ (lines), with $v$ read
off the well-specified cells. The bias is predicted in closed form, not fitted to these points.}
\end{figure}

\noindent
The estimator assumes a first-order Markov chain, and the honest question is how wrong that may be
before the advantage goes. Each departure is Gaussian, so its score is exact and every arm is
scored against the truth of the perturbed model rather than against the chain. Adding an order
(\textsc{ar}(2)) or making the chain inhomogeneous (a site-dependent $\corr$) reverses the ordering
inside the swept range, at $\misBreakLoArtwoD$--$\misBreakHiArtwoD$ and
$\misBreakLoSitevarD$--$\misBreakHiSitevarD$ respectively. A rank-one global latent and a
long-range precision coupling never reverse it: the first is absorbed by the fitted $\corr$, the
second decays to a tie at the widest coupling swept. The two sample sizes separate the two failure
modes exactly as the theory says they should --- at zero strength the error halves between
$\nseq=512$ and $2048$, which is estimation; past the break-even it does not move at all, which is
bias, and no amount of data will move it.
\comp{Chapters 18, 19}

\section{Does score accuracy become sample quality}

\input{../paper/sections/tab-generation}

\noindent
Driven through the same reverse diffusion on identical Brownian paths, so that the only difference
between the arms is the score, the fitted kernel produces sequences with innovation excess kurtosis
$\genKurtgenBEm$ against the reference sample's $\genKurtrefB$ --- close to what the \emph{exact}
score itself achieves under the same sampler ($\genKurtgenBExact$), so most of the residue is the
discretisation of the reverse process and not the estimate. Its log-likelihood gap under the true
law is $\genLoglikgapBEm$ against the exact arm's $\genLoglikgapBExact$. The covariance-matched
score gives $\genKurtgenBLmmse$ --- an essentially Gaussian chain, the fourth-order structure gone
altogether --- at a gap of $\genLoglikgapBLmmse$, and the isotropic score $\genLoglikgapBIso$.
Score error and sample quality move together, which is what licenses reading the rest of the paper
as being about generative models and not only about an estimation problem.
\comp{Chapters 9, 21}

\section{Where the evidence stands}

Every number above is a macro read from a run whose output directory is pinned by SHA-256 in its
registry entry, together with the commit that produced it and the seeds it used; the manuscript
refuses to build if a generated number disagrees with the artefact it came from, and a second,
independent recomputation of every headline number runs beside it. The confirmatory experiments
are frozen: the information decay and its constants, the capacity sweep and its infinite-data
reference, the six-architecture comparison with its screen, generation, misspecification, the
quadrature envelope, the regime sweeps in both capacity and the matched comparison, and the
platykurtic cell. One run is still finishing on the cluster --- the sample-complexity ladder, whose
last two cells of four hundred and eighty are recomputing --- and one paragraph waits on it.
