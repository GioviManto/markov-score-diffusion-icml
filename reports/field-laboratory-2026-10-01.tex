\section*{Summary}

Jérôme and Marc asked for a test of the estimator on Markov data from a physical model: spatial
snapshots of the one-dimensional stochastic Allen--Cahn ($\phi^4$) field, whose stationary law is a
nearest-neighbour Gibbs measure, with four arms tracked across temperature and lattice spacing (a
learned pair energy, the Gaussian closure, the window CNN, the oracle score), and the suggestion that
with this concrete example the paper may be complete. Everything in this report was built, tested
and registered on 1 October; by 5 October every registered run had landed, been read against its
predictions and been frozen (\S\ref{sec:conf}).

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.15em]
\item \st{The chain is exact and in the package} (\S\ref{sec:chain}). Its kernel is a Gaussian step
  tilted by a function of the \emph{arrival} point, the column normaliser being the ground-state
  transform. The Gaussian linear chain is the one member the two laboratories share
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:embed}); the paper's mixture kernel is misspecified here through a nonlinear
  drift, not through the shape of its driving noise.
\item \st{The learned pair energy is a transition kernel} (\S\ref{sec:est}): concave M-step, the
  gradient is Boltzmann learning's positive phase minus a \emph{conditional} negative phase, no
  chain-wide partition function (Proposition~\ref{prop:boltz}).
\item \st{The directed kernel and the symmetric pair energy are one model class}
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:rev}, proved this afternoon): every tilted walk is reversible, with invariant
  law $gZ$ and site energy $f=-\tfrac12\log(g/Z)$, which for the field is the potential. A fitted
  kernel therefore reads as physics, $\hat\Th=\hat\gamma s^2/h$ and $\hat V$ from $\hat f$
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:read}); and the parsimonious exactly specified model parametrises $f$ itself: the
  quartic site energy, five parameters, Jérôme's $W_\theta$ literally (\S\ref{sec:pair}). It joins E33
  as a fifth arm under a registered amendment with two further predictions.
\item \st{One thing in the email does not survive} (\S\ref{sec:closure}): moment projection of the
  messages is not a well-posed estimator on this chain, because the backward message is nearly flat.
  Measured: 42--100\,\% of a projected message's mass at the grid edge. The Gaussian-closure arm is
  therefore the covariance twin, which is what Proposition~1 of the paper proves projection
  computes on linear chains.
\item \st{E32} (\S\ref{sec:e32}): at matched correlation $\lambda_1/\lambda_0=0.85$ the linear floor
  is $0.269$ at $\Th=0.3$ against $0.168$ for the Laplace laboratory, and falls to $0.041$ at
  $\Th=2$; the operating grid's score agrees with a four times finer grid's to $10^{-12}$; Theorem~2's gap ratio rises monotonically
  through $t_*$ to $0.73$--$0.94$ at $t=3$.
\item \st{E33 and E34} (\S\ref{sec:e33}): five kernel arms through grid BP, the window head and the
  transformer under both interfaces, the oracles; the physical statistics the consolidated note
  asks for; the clean-data interface. Eleven predictions were registered before any confirmatory
  cell ran; one Legendre fit crashed the kernel jobs on the finest lattice, and amendment~3 (a failed fit
  is a row counted against the prediction, never a crash) was registered before any kernel row was read.
\item \st{The confirmatory results} (\S\ref{sec:conf}). The five-parameter site energy is the best
  estimator on every cell at every size: through the noise it reaches $0.03$--$0.17$ of the linear floor at
  $M=2048$ and beats the $25$-parameter spline by $43$--$59\,\%$; it reads the temperature within $10\,\%$
  in $96/96$ fits (spread $0.3$--$2.3\,\%$) and the barrier within $20\,\%$ in $78/80$; its samples reproduce
  the kink density and domain length within $5.3\,\%$ and $2.9\,\%$. The paper's mixture kernel converges onto
  the linear floor (within $2\,\%$ at $M=2048$ on every cell), and its samples have up to $1.7$ times too many
  kinks. The window head and the transformer are $4$ to $27$ times the spline's risk on every cell and, at
  $\Th\ge0.5$, worse than the linear floor. The Legendre tilt is unreliable: it parks its invariant law
  outside the data, is sometimes reducible on the grid, and failed $39$ fits on the finest lattice. Under a
  global sign latent (E35) the first-order kernel is worse than the linear floor and the same kernel on the
  augmented state is $8.6$ and $17.9$ times better. Of E33's eight predictions, P1, P3, P4 and P6 are
  confirmed, P8 holds for the temperature and misses the barrier on one cell, and P2, P5 and P7 are falsified
  as written, P7 because the site energy did better than predicted.
\end{enumerate}

Notation (one table for the code, the paper and this report: \code{docs/NOTATION.md}). $\Th$ is the physical
temperature (the code and the cell names call it \code{T}), $h$ the lattice spacing, $\phi_i$ the field and
$a_i=\phi_i/s$ its standardised value, $t$ the diffusion time of the channel $x_t=e^{-t}a+\sqrt{\Delta_t}\,z$ with
$\Delta_t=1-e^{-2t}$, $L$ the number of sites, $M$ the number of training sequences, $N_g$ the grid size. The
consolidated note's $\sigma$ is our $\sigma_t=\sqrt{e^{2t}-1}$ (its channel $Y=X+\sigma Z$ is ours multiplied by
$e^{t}$). $K(a'\mid a)$ is always a normalised transition and $\mathsf T=e^{-W}$ the transfer operator; $\ell_i$ is
the local likelihood, $\overrightarrow m_i$ and $\overleftarrow m_i$ the forward and backward messages without it
(the note's $\alpha_i=\overrightarrow m_i\ell_i$ and $\beta_i=\overleftarrow m_i$); $\gamma$ is the bond variance
and $\xi$ the correlation length. A linear chain $a_{i+1}=\rho a_i+\varepsilon_i$ has driving noise $\varepsilon_i$
(the paper used to say ``innovation''). \emph{Grid BP} is sum-product on $N_g=401$ trapezoidal points on $[-8,8]$:
exact for the finite-state model the grid defines, an approximation of the continuous chain whose truncation and
quadrature errors are measured (E32 P1); run with the true kernel it gives the \emph{reference} (oracle) score.

\section{The chain}\label{sec:chain}

\subsection{From the SPDE to a Markov chain}

The equation $\partial_\tau\phi=\partial_x^2\phi-V'(\phi)+\sqrt{2\Th}\,\eta$ with $V(\phi)=(\phi^2-1)^2/4$
is the gradient flow of $H[\phi]=\int[\tfrac12(\partial_x\phi)^2+V(\phi)]\dd x$ at temperature
$\Th$; in one space dimension its stationary law is the Gibbs measure $\propto\exp(-H/\Th)$. On a
lattice of spacing $h$, $H_h(\phi)=\sum_i[(\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i)^2/2h+hV(\phi_i)]$, a nearest-neighbour
energy. A spatial snapshot is therefore a first-order Markov chain in the site index. Writing the
site terms symmetrically,
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf T(\phi,\phi')=D(\phi)\,G(\phi'-\phi)\,D(\phi'),\qquad
  D=e^{-hV/2\Th},\quad G(u)=e^{-u^2/2\Th h},
  \label{eq:T}
\end{equation}
is a symmetric, positive kernel, Hilbert--Schmidt for a confining $V$, with a simple largest
eigenvalue $\lambda_0$ and a positive eigenfunction $\psi_0$, $\int\psi_0^2=1$.

\begin{proposition}[ground-state transform]\label{prop:gs}
$K(\phi'\mid\phi)=\mathsf T(\phi,\phi')\psi_0(\phi')/(\lambda_0\psi_0(\phi))$ is a Markov kernel;
$\pi=\psi_0^2$ is invariant for it; and the chain is reversible, $\pi(\phi)K(\phi'\mid\phi)=\pi(\phi')K(\phi\mid\phi')$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\int K(\phi'\mid\phi)\dd\phi'=\int\mathsf T(\phi,\phi')\psi_0(\phi')\dd\phi'/(\lambda_0\psi_0(\phi))=1$ by the
eigen-equation. $\int\pi(\phi)K(\phi'\mid\phi)\dd\phi=\psi_0(\phi')\int\mathsf T(\phi,\phi')\psi_0(\phi)\dd\phi/\lambda_0=\psi_0(\phi')^2$
by symmetry of $\mathsf T$ and the eigen-equation again. $\pi(\phi)K(\phi'\mid\phi)=\psi_0(\phi)\mathsf T(\phi,\phi')\psi_0(\phi')/\lambda_0$
is symmetric in $(\phi,\phi')$.
\end{proof}
The infinite-volume Gibbs measure restricted to $L$ consecutive sites is this stationary chain,
$\pi(\phi_1)\prod K(\phi_{i+1}\mid\phi_i)$; that is what ``spatial snapshots of a stationary field''
means, and it is the law the data are drawn from. (A finite open chain with free ends is also
Markov but inhomogeneous within a correlation length of its ends; we do not use it.) The lag-$k$
covariance is $\sum_{n\ge1}(\lambda_n/\lambda_0)^k\langle\psi_0|\phi|\psi_n\rangle^2$, so $\rho_1:=\lambda_1/\lambda_0$
plays the role $\rho$ plays for the linear chains and the correlation length in sites is $\xi=1/\log(1/\rho_1)$.
In one dimension there is no phase transition at any $\Th>0$: a kink costs the finite energy $E_{\rm kink}$, so
$\rho_1<1$ and $\xi$ is finite at every temperature, growing roughly like $e^{E_{\rm kink}/\Th}$ as $\Th\to0$. The
low-temperature cells sit in a sharp crossover, not in an ordered phase.

\subsection{The tilted-walk identity}

\begin{proposition}[tilted walk]\label{prop:tilt}
With $g:=D\psi_0$,
\begin{equation}
  K(\phi'\mid\phi)=\frac{G(\phi'-\phi)\,g(\phi')}{Z(\phi)},\qquad
  Z(\phi)=\int G(\phi'-\phi)g(\phi')\dd\phi'=\frac{\lambda_0\psi_0(\phi)}{D(\phi)}.
  \label{eq:tilt}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:T}, $\mathsf T(\phi,\phi')\psi_0(\phi')=D(\phi)G(\phi'-\phi)g(\phi')$, and the eigen-equation
reads $D(\phi)\int G(\phi'-\phi)g(\phi')\dd\phi'=\lambda_0\psi_0(\phi)$, which is the stated $Z$. Substituting in
Proposition~\ref{prop:gs}'s $K$ gives \eqref{eq:tilt}.
\end{proof}
So the kernel is a Gaussian random-walk step of variance $\Th h$, tilted by a positive function of
the arrival point and normalised per column: Jérôme's ``Gaussian increment tilted by
$\psi_0(\phi')e^{-hV(\phi')/\Th}/\psi_0(\phi)$'', with the normaliser made explicit. The point for the
estimator is that \emph{the column normaliser is the ground-state transform}: a family that
parametrises $\log g$ and normalises columns on the grid contains the truth exactly, and never needs
$\psi_0$ or $\lambda_0$.

\subsection{Where the two laboratories meet}

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian embedding]\label{prop:embed}
The linear-Gaussian kernel $\Nn(a';\rho a,q)$ with $0<\rho<1$ is the member of \eqref{eq:tilt} with
step variance $\gamma=q/\rho$ and $\log g(a')=-(1-\rho)a'^2/2q+\mathrm{const}$. No other kernel of
the linear form $p_\varepsilon(a'-\rho a)$, $\rho\neq0$, belongs to the family, and no other member of
the family has state-independent driving noise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\log\Nn(a';\rho a,q)=-a'^2/2q+\rho aa'/q-\rho^2a^2/2q+\mathrm{c}$ and $\log[G_\gamma(a'-a)g(a')]=-a'^2/2\gamma+aa'/\gamma-a^2/2\gamma+\log g(a')+\mathrm c$.
Matching the cross term gives $\gamma=q/\rho$; the $a'^2$ terms give $\log g(a')=-a'^2(1/q-\rho/q)/2$,
a Gaussian of variance $q/(1-\rho)=1+\rho$; the $a^2$ terms are absorbed by $Z(a)$. For the second
claim, $\partial_a\partial_{a'}\log K$ equals $1/\gamma$, a constant, for every member of \eqref{eq:tilt},
and equals $-\rho(\log p_\varepsilon)''(a'-\rho a)$ for a linear kernel, which is constant iff
$\log p_\varepsilon$ is quadratic.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the misspecification mechanism]
Expanding $\log g$ around $a$, $K(a'\mid a)\approx\Nn\!\big(a';\,a+\gamma(\log g)'(a),\,\gamma\big)$:
at fine $h$ the chain is the Euler step of $\dd a=\gamma(\log g)'(a)\dd\tau+\sqrt{2\gamma}\dd W_\tau$ with a
\emph{nonlinear} drift. The mixture kernel $\sum_c\pi_c\Nn(a'-\rho a;\mu_c,s_c^2)$ has a
linear drift and state-independent driving noise; it is misspecified here through the drift. That is
a different mechanism from every axis of the paper's misspecification study (global latent,
long-range coupling, a second lag, site-dependent $\rho$, heteroskedasticity), and E33's P1/P2 measure it.
\end{remark}

\section{The estimator: the learned pair energy as a kernel}\label{sec:est}

The family is $K_\theta(a'\mid a)=\Nn(a';a,\gamma)\exp(B(a')\theta)/Z_\theta(a)$ with $B$ a linear basis
for $\log g$ (Legendre polynomials on $[-A,A]$, or cubic B-splines), the constant function omitted
(it is absorbed by $Z$), and $Z$ the column normaliser under the grid's trapezoid weights, exactly as
the MDN kernel is normalised. Parameters $(\log\gamma,\theta)$. In Jérôme's language, the pair energy is
$W_\theta(a,a')=(a'-a)^2/2\gamma-\log g_\theta(a')$ and the transfer-matrix normaliser is $Z_\theta(a)$.

\begin{lemma}[the M-step is concave in the tilt]
With $\Xi_{kj}$ the expected transition mass on $u_j\to u_k$ from the E-step and $N_j=\sum_k\Xi_{kj}$,
$Q(\theta)=\langle\Xi,\log K_\theta\rangle=\sum_k(\sum_j\Xi_{kj})B_k\theta-\sum_jN_j\log\sum_kw_k e^{B_k\theta+c_{kj}}$,
linear minus a sum of log-sum-exp functions, hence concave in $\theta$ at fixed $\gamma$; its gradient is
$\nabla_\theta Q=B^{\top}\!\sum_j\tilde\Xi_{\cdot j}$ with $\tilde\Xi_{kj}=\Xi_{kj}-N_jp_j(k)$, $p_j$ the model's
column distribution, and $\partial Q/\partial\log\gamma=\sum_{kj}\tilde\Xi_{kj}[(u_k-u_j)^2/2\gamma-\tfrac12]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[what Boltzmann learning becomes for a kernel]\label{prop:boltz}
By Fisher's identity the exact gradient of the noisy-data log-likelihood is $\langle\Xi,\nabla\log K_\theta\rangle$,
which for this family is
\[
  \nabla\mathcal L=\sum_j\Big[\sum_k\Xi_{kj}\,\nabla(-W_\theta(u_j,u_k))-N_j\,\E_{K_\theta(\cdot\mid u_j)}\!\big[\nabla(-W_\theta(u_j,\cdot))\big]\Big]:
\]
the positive phase is the posterior pair expectation (as in the note's \S16.2) and the negative
phase is the kernel's own \emph{conditional} expectation at each source state, weighted by the
posterior source mass. The undirected pair-energy model of the note's \S16 has instead the chain's
\emph{equilibrium} pair marginal as negative phase and a chain-wide $\log Z_\theta$. Both are exact
maximum likelihood for their model; the directed form needs no $Z_\theta$, its column normalisers are
the ground-state transform (Proposition~\ref{prop:tilt}), and it contains the field's kernel exactly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[every tilted walk is reversible]\label{prop:rev}
Let $\gamma>0$ and $g>0$ with $Z_g(a)=\int\Nn(a';a,\gamma)g(a')\dd a'<\infty$, and let
$K_g(a'\mid a)=\Nn(a';a,\gamma)g(a')/Z_g(a)$. Then $\pi_g\propto gZ_g$ is invariant and $K_g$ is reversible
with respect to it. Equivalently $K_g$ is the ground-state transform of the symmetric operator
$\mathsf T_g(a,a')=u(a)\Nn(a';a,\gamma)u(a')$ with $u=\sqrt{g/Z_g}$, whose Perron eigenfunction is
$\psi=\sqrt{gZ_g}$ with eigenvalue $1$; so $K_g$ is the Gibbs chain of the pair energy
\begin{equation}
  W(a,a')=\frac{(a'-a)^2}{2\gamma}+f(a)+f(a'),\qquad f=-\tfrac12\log(g/Z_g),
  \label{eq:W}
\end{equation}
and conversely every pair energy of this form is a tilted walk. For the field's $(\gamma,g)$ of
\eqref{eq:tilt}, $f(a)=(h/2\Th)V(sa)+\mathrm{const}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\pi_g(a)K_g(a'\mid a)\propto g(a)g(a')\Nn(a';a,\gamma)$ is symmetric in $(a,a')$: detailed balance, and
invariance by integrating over $a$. For the second form, $(\mathsf T_g\psi)(a)=u(a)\int\Nn(a';a,\gamma)
u(a')\psi(a')\dd a'=u(a)\int\Nn(a';a,\gamma)g(a')\dd a'=u(a)Z_g(a)=\psi(a)$ since $u\psi=g$; and
$\mathsf T_g(a,a')\psi(a')/\psi(a)=\Nn(a';a,\gamma)\,g(a')/Z_g(a)$ since $u\psi=g$ and $\psi/u=Z_g$. The converse is
Proposition~\ref{prop:tilt} with $D=e^{-f}$. For the field, $Z_g=\lambda_0\psi_0/D$ by \eqref{eq:tilt}, so
$g/Z_g=D^2/\lambda_0$ and $f=-\log D+\mathrm{const}=(h/2\Th)V+\mathrm{const}$.
\end{proof}
Numerically, on the $(401,8)$ grid: $\pi_g$ by the formula and by an eigensolve of $K_g$ agree to
$4\times10^{-12}$, detailed balance holds to $10^{-14}$, and the site energy of the field's own kernel
equals $(h/2\Th)V(sa)$ to $2\times10^{-10}$ (\code{tests/test\_pair\_energy\_kernel.py}). The first
question put to Jérôme and Marc in the morning version of this report (directed kernel or symmetric
pair energy?) dissolves: no tilted walk is irreversible, and the symmetric form has the same single
free function. What differs is the algorithm, per-column normalisers against the Perron eigenvalue.

\begin{corollary}[a fitted kernel reads as physics]\label{cor:read}
In standardised units $\gamma=\Th h/s^2$ and $f(a)=(h/2\Th)V(sa)+\mathrm{const}$. A fit $(\hat\gamma,\hat g)$
of any kernel of this class gives
\[
  \hat\Th=\hat\gamma s^2/h,\qquad \hat V(\phi)=\frac{2\hat\Th}{h}\,\hat f(\phi/s)+\mathrm{const},\qquad
  \hat f=-\tfrac12\log\big(\hat g/Z_{\hat g}\big):
\]
the temperature from the bond variance, the one parameter that enters no tilt; the potential, its
barrier and its wells, from the site energy. Moreover the conditional mean step is exactly
$\gamma(\log Z_g)'(a)$ (exponential tilting), which for the field is $\Th h(\log\psi_0)'+(h^2/2)V'$ in
physical units, so the small-$h$ limit is $\dd\phi=\Th(\log\psi_0)'\dd x+\sqrt{\Th}\,\dd B$: the drift of
the stationary field in the site index is the log-derivative of the ground state, not $-V'$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The physicist's parametrisation}\label{sec:pair}
Since $\log g=\log\psi_f-f$ is a transcendental function of the potential while $f$ itself is a
quartic, the parsimonious exactly specified model parametrises $f$. \code{PairEnergyKernel}
(\code{src/markov\_score/pair\_energy\_kernel.py}): $f(a)=\sum_{m\le4}c_m(a/2)^m$ and $\log\gamma$, five
parameters, with the same fixed soft wall as an energy; $K_f$ by one dense eigensolve of $\mathsf T_f$
per evaluation (15\,ms on 401 points), invariant law $\psi_f^2$ in closed form; the M-step maximises
$Q=\langle\Xi,\log K_f\rangle$ by L-BFGS-B with the analytic gradient of the Perron eigenvector from
first-order perturbation theory in the complete discretised eigenbasis,
$\partial_{c_m}\psi_0=-\sum_{n\ge1}\psi_n\,\frac{\lambda_0+\lambda_n}{\lambda_0-\lambda_n}\langle\psi_n\mid B_m\psi_0\rangle$,
checked against finite differences and Fisher's identity to $10^{-4}$. $Q$ is not concave in $c$, so a
step is accepted only if $Q$ did not decrease, as for every other kernel. With the field's own
$(\gamma,c)$ the kernel reproduces the chain's to $2\times10^{-3}$ (the coarse eigensolve against the
fine one), and from 1024 clean chains at $(\Th,h)=(0.3,1)$ it reads $\hat\Th$ within $5\,\%$ and the
barrier within $10\,\%$. This is literally the pair energy $W_\theta$ of the email, its Boltzmann learning
written as a kernel. It joins E33 as a fifth arm, \code{em\_pair\_quartic}, under amendment~1 of the
registry (predictions P7 and P8, declared before any E33 kernel result existed), run as its own
shards so the four registered arms' tables are never touched. In the smoke run at $M=32$ it already
sits at $0.13$ against the polynomial tilt's $0.25$: five parameters have the smaller variance.

Implementation (\code{src/markov\_score/tilted\_kernel.py}): L-BFGS-B on $(\log\gamma,\theta)$ with the
analytic gradient, warm-started, accepted only if $Q$ did not decrease (a rejected step returns the
receiver's parameters in a new object, so the checkpoint ladder stays honest); Fisher's identity
checked against finite differences of the evidence to $10^{-5}$; the Gaussian embedding recovered
on a Gaussian chain, and the field's kernel recovered from clean data with the spline basis
(\code{tests/test\_tilted\_kernel.py}). Two details earned by the first smoke run:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item \st{The tilt carries a fixed soft wall} $-(a/5)^{12}$ on the $(401,8)$ grid. The data occupy
  $|a|<4$, so the basis beyond that is constrained only through far columns' normalisers, and a free
  fit can raise the tilt at the grid edge and create a metastable pocket there, invisible to the
  likelihood but the place where the kernel's invariant law then puts mass. The wall is $0.002$ at
  $|a|=3$, $0.07$ at $4$ and $280$ at $8$, more than any coefficient within the optimiser's bounds can
  undo; the field's true tilt is already $-0.2a^4$ there.
\item \st{The initial law is the kernel's own invariant density}, by a direct eigensolve of the
  $401\times401$ operator (\code{em.fit\_em} takes a callable \code{log\_mu}): the model is a stationary
  chain, as the data are. The M-step maximises the edge term; any evidence decrease is recorded.
\end{itemize}

\section{What does not extend: Gaussian closure of the messages}\label{sec:closure}

The paper's Proposition~1 identifies moment-projected sum-product with the LMMSE of the covariance
twin, and its proof uses the linear transition. On the field chain the projected recursion is not a
well-posed estimator, for a reason that was found by the diagnostic, not anticipated.

\begin{proposition}[the backward message saturates]
Let $\log g(a')\le -c\,a'^4+C$ (a super-Gaussian tilt, $c>0$). Then $K(\cdot\mid a)$ concentrates, as
$|a|\to\infty$, near $a'_*(a)$ with $|a'_*|\sim(|a|/4c\gamma)^{1/3}$, and the backward message
$\overleftarrow m(a)=\int K(a'\mid a)\ell(a')\dd a'=\E[\ell(a_{i+1})\mid a_i=a]$ is bounded and decays only as
$\exp(-c'|a|^{2/3})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The exponent $-(a'-a)^2/2\gamma-ca'^4$ is maximised where $(a-a')/\gamma=4ca'^3$, i.e.\ $a'_*\approx(a/4c\gamma)^{1/3}$
for large $|a|$, with curvature $\sim12ca'^2_*$, so $K(\cdot\mid a)$ is a sharpening Gaussian at $a'_*$ and
$\overleftarrow m(a)\approx\ell(a'_*(a))$; for the channel's $\ell(a')\propto\exp[-(x-e^{-t}a')^2/2\Delta_t]$ this is $\exp[-(x-e^{-t}a'_*)^2/2\Delta_t]$.
\end{proof}
So on a finite grid $\overleftarrow m$ is nearly constant, and the Gaussian with its moments is close to flat: the
projection discards the backward information. Measured at $t=0.47$ on the $(401,8)$ grid, 28\,\% of
a projected message's mass sits in the outer 5\,\% of the grid; over E32's cells and levels the
maximum is 42\,\% at the base cell and 98--100\,\% at every other cell (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). On
grids wide enough for the Gaussian and Laplace chains' backward messages (half-width 32 at $t=1$)
the projected recursion reproduces the LMMSE to $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-5}$, so this is a property of the
field, not of the implementation (\code{tests/test\_bp\_projected.py}).

Consequently the Gaussian-closure arm of the field chain is its covariance twin, the LMMSE of the
true covariance, by the identification Proposition~1 proves on linear chains; the projected
recursion is kept as the diagnostic and its edge mass is recorded. Jérôme's expectation that closure
``fails badly'' by smearing across domain walls is then measured by the linear floor, which is what
the floor has always measured, and it does: $0.269$ at the base cell. For the paper this is a
scope sentence on Proposition~1 and one sentence in the field section.

the barrier and the marginal is near-Gaussian, so the data carry almost no information about the
shape of $V$ between the wells; the risk is nonetheless $0.019$ against the closure's $0.041$,
because what the score needs there is the bond and the curvature, which are identified. P8 was
written to expect this. Third, the clean-data fits read everything to $0.5\,\%$ at the base cell:
the price of the channel at $M=2048$ is a factor of a few in risk, not in the physics.

One negative result came with the preview (N-33 in \code{docs/NEGATIVE\_RESULTS.md}, corrected on
2 October): the \emph{polynomial} tilt sometimes fails badly, with risks of $0.18$ to $1.79$ in five of its
twenty preview fits, clean and noisy. The cause is not where the fit puts its transitions but where
it puts its \emph{invariant law}. The likelihood constrains $\log g$ only where data arrive
($|a|\lesssim3.5$); beyond that, a global basis can raise the tilt and create a pocket, and in every failed fit
the kernel's own invariant law sits entirely in that pocket. Since the estimator uses that law for the
first site, every posterior is dragged outside the data. In the other fifteen Legendre fits, and in all
fifty-two spline and site-energy fits (the previews and E36), the mass beyond $|a|=3.5$ is at most $1.1\times10^{-4}$. That mass, not the
coefficient bound first suspected, is the diagnostic: E33's amendment~2 reports the number of such
``parked'' fits beside every verdict, before any confirmatory row exists. The role of the parsimonious
exactly specified kernel passes to the site energy, which confines by construction.

\subsection{E45: inpainting with posterior uncertainty}

The note's section 9.7 asks for posterior sampling, and a missing site is a site with no likelihood. E45 hides part of a noisy sequence (a random half, or a gap of $8$ or $16$ sites; noise level $t=0.5$) and asks what E33's \emph{frozen} fitted kernels do, with no refit: the posterior mean by grid BP and $200$ forward-filter backward-sample draws for the $90\,\%$ interval, against the oracle (the true kernel) and the Gaussian conditional with the true covariance. Three predictions hold and two fail narrowly. The oracle's intervals cover the truth $0.89$ of the time on both cells and all patterns; the learned site energy and the learned spline are indistinguishable from the oracle (error ratio $1.000$, coverage $0.89$, width ratio $1.00$, in all sixteen seeds), so the machinery is sound for missing data. What fails is the expectation that the Gaussian conditional would be visibly worse: on the base cell its hidden-site error is $1.5$, $2.8$ and $4.6\,\%$ above the oracle's for the long gap, the short gap and the random half, its interval is $9\,\%$ wider at the long gap and over-covers ($0.926$, not under-covers; the predicted miscalibration of more than $0.03$ is $+0.026$, the predicted width excess of $10\,\%$ is $9.4\,\%$), and on the $T=1$ cell every arm is within $1\,\%$. The mixture kernel sits with the linear estimator, as in E33. At this noise level the exact nonlinear posterior buys little over a Gaussian smoother; a lower noise level or longer gaps is where a larger gap would be, and was not run.

\subsection{E41 and E42: closing two caveats}

\emph{The neural kernel's budget (E41).} E33's neural kernel ended at its last checkpoint in $149$ of $192$ fits at $M=2048$, so its $2$--$6\times$ gap to the spline could have been a budget artefact. E41 refits it at four times the budget

E33's neural-kernel numbers stand.

\emph{The barrier at high temperature (E42).} In E33 the barrier height read from the site energy was within $20\,\%$ in $14/16$ seeds at $\Theta=1$ but $6/16$ at $\Theta=2$ ($M=2048$). E42 extends the same fits to $M=8192$ and $32768$ on sixteen seeds. At $\Theta=2$ the error keeps falling

\subsection{E43: is the site-energy form wide enough?}

Question 1 below asks whether the paper needs a general pair kernel. E43 fits the most agnostic family there is: a log-linear conditional kernel with a two-dimensional cubic-spline score ($100$ coefficients, exact EM, no penalty; a penalty and a ridge were tried and removed because they break exact EM).
Laboratory A is E33's $\phi^4$ cells, where the site energy is exactly specified: the unrestricted kernel is $2.8\times$ the site energy at $M=128$ and $5.5\times$ at $M=2048$ on the base cell ($2.5\times$ and $3.0\times$ on the $\Theta=1$ cell), so the price of generality \emph{grows} with the data rather than shrinking (predictions P1 and P2 fail),
and it is also worse than the $25$-parameter spline ($1.5$ to $3.1\times$ at $M=2048$) while still far better than the mixture ($0.18$ and $0.42\times$, in $16/16$ seeds). Laboratory B is a chain outside the tilted-walk class, a Laplace bond on a double well calibrated beforehand to the base cell's correlation and kurtosis, with exact samples and an exact oracle:
the site energy and the spline stall at about $0.103$--$0.108$ while the unrestricted kernel keeps improving ($0.121$, $0.080$, $0.068$ at $M=128$, $512$, $2048$), overtaking them between $128$ and $512$ and ending $1.5\times$ below the site energy in $8/8$ seeds. The answer to the question is therefore conditional: keep the five-parameter site energy when the physics is a gradient flow,
and use the unrestricted kernel as a misspecification test, since it only wins once the Gaussian-bond families have stopped improving.

\subsection{E44: non-equilibrium snapshots}

Snapshots taken while a field coarsens after a quench are not Gibbs. E44 evolves the lattice Allen--Cahn dynamics from a small random start (the simulator reproduces the exact chain after a long run: variance $0.997$, lag-1 correlation $0.84$ against $0.848$, kurtosis $-1.34$ against $-1.35$) and trains and tests at $\tau=1.5$, $3$, $6$, $12$ after the quench and at equilibrium
(at this temperature and spacing the field is equilibrium-like by $\tau\approx50$). With no exact score off equilibrium the reference is the clean sequence, so the readings are ratios against the empirical linear estimator. The site energy beats it in $8/8$ seeds at every time, with a ratio that moves smoothly from $0.986$ at $\tau=1.5$ to $0.950$ at equilibrium (compressed by the irreducible noise in this metric);
the tilted spline equals it to three digits at every time, so the predicted flip in their order does not happen (P3 fails; P1, P2 and P4 hold). The Gibbs-class kernels are robust to data of this kind. Not tested: slower quenches (lower temperature, finer spacing).

\subsection{E46: why the augmented kernel gains with no latent}

E35x found the augmented kernel $18$ to $27\,\%$ below the first-order one at $b=0$, where there is no latent. The augmented model is a mixture of the kernel and its \emph{reflection} with shared parameters, so its E-step feeds the kernel every sequence and its mirror image, and the truth is reflection-symmetric.
E46 fits the \emph{first-order} kernel on the sequences and their mirror images, on E35's own data, sixteen seeds. That fit is $32$ and $39\,\%$ below the plain first-order one at $M=512$ and $2048$ (below it in $16/16$ and $15/16$ seeds), and $16$ and $17\,\%$ below the augmented one:
the whole gain is the symmetry, and a first-order fit on the data and their mirror images uses it better than the mixture does (P2 holds; P1, which predicted equality with the augmented arm, fails in this favourable direction). Its seed-mean risk equals that of a fit on twice as many \emph{independent} sequences ($0.0162$ against $0.0161$ at $M=512$, $0.0066$ against $0.0066$ at $2048$), although the registered interval criterion for that clause (P3) fails
because the seed-to-seed variation is large (per-seed ratios $0.10$ to $2.25$).

\section{Numerics}

\st{Eigensolve.} $A=W^{1/2}\mathsf TW^{1/2}$ on 2401 points of $[-12,12]$ (physical units), symmetric,
\code{scipy.linalg.eigh}; $\psi_n=W^{-1/2}v_n$. Components below $10^{-13}$ of the maximum are
eigensolver noise and are carried as $\log\psi_0=-\infty$ (stationary mass below $10^{-26}$); a kernel
column leaving such a state is the normalised $\mathsf T$ column. Spectral accuracy: $|\Delta\lambda_0|/\lambda_0<10^{-10}$,
$|\Delta\rho_1|<10^{-10}$ under halving the spacing and under widening the domain to $[-16,16]$.
Completeness: $\sum_nc_n^2=\mathrm{Var}_\pi(\phi)$ to $10^{-10}$.

\st{Samplers.} Continuum: propose $\phi'\sim\Nn(\phi,\Th h)$, accept with $g(\phi')/\max g$, $\log\psi_0$
by cubic spline on the resolved range; burn-in $\lceil\log(10^{-12})/\log\rho_1\rceil$ steps from
$\Nn(0,s^2)$. Lattice: inverse-CDF on the columns of the discretised kernel. Agreement in variance,
lag-one correlation and kurtosis within Monte Carlo error at 3000 chains.

\st{Fourth cumulant.} $\E[a_ia_ja_ka_l]$ for $i\le j\le k\le l$ equals
$\langle\psi_0|\,a\,R^{j-i}a\,R^{k-j}a\,R^{l-k}a\,|\psi_0\rangle$ with $R=\mathsf T/\lambda_0$, a triple sum over
the leading 256 modes with $\Phi_{nm}=\langle\psi_n|a|\psi_m\rangle$; $\kappa_4$ by Wick subtraction. The
diagonal reproduces the marginal excess kurtosis to $10^{-6}$.
