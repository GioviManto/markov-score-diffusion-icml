{\Large\bfseries Recovering the dynamics of a sequence seen only through noise}\\[0.3em]
{\normalsize Results in brief --- 24 September 2026}
\end{center}

\vspace{-0.4em}
\section*{The question}

A diffusion model learns by adding noise to data and learning to remove it. To remove it, it must
learn one object at each noise level: the \emph{score}. We study a case where the score can be
computed exactly --- data generated by a Markov chain, so the truth is available to compare
against --- and ask two things. What does the noise actually destroy? And is a method that knows
the data has a chain structure better than a general-purpose network that does not, when both are
shown exactly the same information?

\section*{What we found}

\textbf{Noise destroys fine structure far faster than it destroys correlation, at an exact
rate.} This is the theoretical result, and panel (a) is it measured. Each degree of structure
decays at its own exponential rate, so the higher-order detail that makes data non-Gaussian goes
first while correlation survives. Past a certain noise level everything still learnable is
second-order --- and we can say where that level is.

\textbf{Knowing the structure is worth 5--10$\times$ at equal information.} In panel (b) the
chain estimator is ahead in all six regimes and in every one of the sixteen repetitions of each,
by factors from $\rbIsmRatioEvfiveonetwentyeight$ to $\rbIsmRatioEvninefivethirtytwo$. The
comparison is deliberately unflattering to us: the networks are re-tuned separately in each
regime, and our method may not peek at the true answer when deciding when to stop. The advantage
is \emph{smallest} where the chain carries the most non-Gaussian structure, which is the direction
panel (a) predicts.

\textbf{The advantage is fourth-order structure and nothing else.} Panel (c) is the control.
Restricted to Gaussian randomness, the chain model lands exactly on the second-order floor
($\lmmseFloorLaplace$) and stays there however much data it gets; given the capacity for
non-Gaussian structure it goes far below. The same holds in fifteen further configurations,
the restricted model matching the theoretical floor to $\regIdentMax$ in the worst of them, and
when the non-Gaussianity runs the \emph{other} way, on a chain less heavy-tailed than Gaussian:
the factor there is
$\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwo$ [$\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwolo$,
$\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwohi$].

\section*{Where it stops working, and what it rests on}

The estimator assumes a first-order chain, and we measured how wrong that may be before the
advantage goes. Two departures reverse the ordering inside the range swept --- a second order of
memory, and a correlation varying along the sequence --- at strengths
$\misBreakLoArtwoD$--$\misBreakHiArtwoD$ and $\misBreakLoSitevarD$--$\misBreakHiSitevarD$; two
others never reverse it. We report the boundary rather than bury it.

is still on the cluster; nothing above depends on it. One correction is on the record: the capacity
sweep in the earlier archival study is void --- its training draw left every configuration fitted to
a single sequence --- and the capacity evidence above is independent of it.
