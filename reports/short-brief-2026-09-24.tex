\section*{1.\quad What is being measured}

\subsection{The setting} A diffusion model generates data by learning to undo noise, and the
object it must learn at each noise level is the \emph{score}. On real data the true score is
unknown, so nobody can measure how far a learned one is from it. We work in a case where the true
score is computable exactly, and ask one question: \textbf{is an estimator that knows the data has
a sequential structure better than a general network that does not, when both are shown the same
information?}

\subsection{The data} A Markov chain: each site is a fraction of the previous one plus fresh
randomness,
\[
a_{i+1} \;=\; \corr\,a_i + \varepsilon_i ,\qquad i = 1,\dots,\nsites-1 .
\]
$\corr$ is the \textbf{correlation}: how strongly each site remembers the one before it. The
innovation $\varepsilon$ is drawn from a density proportional to $e^{-|x/s|^{\beta}}$, and the
exponent $\beta$ is the \textbf{shape}. The scale $s$ is always reset so the variance of
$\varepsilon$ is the same for every $\beta$: $\beta$ therefore changes how the randomness is
\emph{distributed}, never how large it is. This is what separates ``non-Gaussian structure'' from
``more noise'', and it is the distinction the whole result turns on.

\subsection{Our estimator, and its one dial} We fit the chain by EM: alternate between inferring
what the clean sequence probably was (exactly, by message passing) and re-estimating the
parameters. The innovation law is \emph{not} assumed --- it is fitted as a mixture of $\ncomp$
Gaussians with free weights, means and variances, which can represent heavy tails, skew and
bimodality alike. It is never told the truth is Laplace, and at $\ncomp=16$ it recovers an excess
kurtosis of $\capKurtsixteenD$ against the true $3.00$. Setting $\ncomp=1$ forces the innovation
Gaussian; that restriction is the control in \S3.

\subsection{The competitors} Six neural sequence denoisers: a fully connected network, a
convolutional window head, a dilated convolutional network (TCN), a bidirectional GRU, a
transformer with relative-position attention, and a 1-D U-Net. Each is screened and tuned
\emph{separately inside every regime}, and the best of the six in each cell is what we report.

\subsection{The rule of the comparison} Both sides see \textbf{one noisy view of each sequence and
nothing else} --- never the clean sequence, never a second view. Neither side may use the true
score to decide when to stop training: the networks stop on a held-out noisy objective, our
estimator on held-out evidence. This is the condition comparisons of this kind most often violate,
and it is the one that makes the numbers below a fair fight.

\subsection{The measure} Every number in this document is a \textbf{relative score error},
\[
\sqrt{\;\textstyle\sum \lVert \hat s - s_{\text{true}}\rVert^2 \;\Big/\; \sum \lVert s_{\text{true}}\rVert^2\;}
\]
summed over the twelve noise levels and the test sequences: the size of your error divided by the
size of the thing you are estimating. It is dimensionless and has three landmarks. The exact score
scores $0$. Ignoring the sequence structure entirely scores $\isoFloorLaplace$. And
$\lmmseFloorLaplace$ is the best score reachable by \emph{any} method that uses correlations but
nothing beyond them --- a proven floor, not an empirical best, which is what lets us say what a
method has learned rather than only which one won.

\subsection{How to read it} Our estimator is ahead in all six regimes and in every one of the
sixteen repetitions of each, by factors from $\rbIsmRatioEvfiveonetwentyeight$ to
$\rbIsmRatioEvninefivethirtytwo$. Three things in the table matter beyond that headline. First,
\emph{ev.} and \emph{bp} are nearly identical, so being denied the true score when choosing where
to stop costs us almost nothing --- the two rules pick the same iteration in
\rbEmAgreeRungTotal{} cells. Second, the conservatism runs against us: our estimator is still
improving when its iteration budget runs out in \rbEmEvAtCapTotal{} cells, so its error there is an
upper bound. Third, the floor itself moves with $\corr$, and where the network sits relative to it
is the real story: at $\corr=0.95$ the network ($\rbIsmBestninefivethirtytwo$) is \emph{worse} than the floor ($\rbLmmseninefivethirtytwo$),
meaning one noisy view per sequence is not enough for it to learn even the correlation, while at
$\corr=0.5$ it is below the floor and has genuinely learned beyond second order.

\section*{3.\quad The control, and the boundary}

\subsection{What the margin is made of} Panel (b) and the table above are a comparison of our
estimator with \emph{itself}, one dial moved. Restricted to a Gaussian innovation it lands on the
second-order floor and stays there however much data it is given; allowed a non-Gaussian
innovation it goes far below. Since nothing else differs, the margin is fourth-order structure and
cannot be anything else. The same holds across fifteen further combinations of $(\corr,\nsites)$,
with the restricted arm matching the theoretical floor to $\regIdentMax$ in the worst of them ---
which incidentally tests the grid, the message passing, the EM and the scoring at once. It also
holds with the non-Gaussianity reversed, on a chain \emph{less} heavy-tailed than Gaussian, where
the factor is $\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwo$
[$\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwolo$, $\rbBimodalIsmRatioEveightfivethirtytwohi$].

\subsection{Two things to notice in it} At $\nseq=32$ the richest kernel ($\capEmsixteenA$) is \emph{worse}
than the simplest non-Gaussian one ($\capEmtwoA$): flexibility costs variance when data is short, and
the ordering only reverses by $\nseq=2048$. And the last column is the mixture closest in
Kullback--Leibler divergence to the innovation law itself, which is a reference for how expressive
the family is --- not a bound on score error, and not comparable with the finite-$\nseq$ columns.

\subsection{Where it ends} Two departures reverse the ordering inside the range swept: a second
order of memory between $\misBreakLoArtwoD$ and $\misBreakHiArtwoD$, and a drifting correlation
between $\misBreakLoSitevarD$ and $\misBreakHiSitevarD$. Two never reverse it --- a shared hidden
factor is absorbed into the fitted correlation, and a long-range coupling decays to a tie. The
limitation to state plainly: all four perturbations are Gaussian, because that is what makes the
perturbed truth's score exact, so this locates the boundary against departures in the
\emph{dependency structure} and not against departures in the shape of the noise.

\subsection{What the numbers rest on} Every experiment is frozen with hash-pinned outputs and a
configuration registered before it ran. Each number is generated from those outputs by one tool
and recomputed independently by a second that fails if the two disagree; the figures check
themselves against the manuscript's numbers and refuse to be written on a mismatch. One correction
is on the record: the capacity sweep in the earlier archival study is void --- its training draw
left every configuration fitted to a single repeated sequence --- and the capacity evidence above
was computed independently of it.
