\Cref{tab:baselines} compares six architectures trained under one recipe (interface~A:
per-example noise level, batch $64$, Adam with clipping, both output parameterisations,
checkpoint selected on validation), each with the configuration that won its family's
screen on the development seeds (\cref{tab:screen}, \cref{app:protocol}), on the test
bundles of the capacity experiment. Three things separate them. Weight sharing separates
the fully connected network from the rest at every size ($\baseWorstA$ against
$\baseBestA$ at $\nseq=32$). Locality is the better prior when sequences are scarce---the
\baseBestArchA{} is the best network at $\nseq=32$---and stops paying once they are not:
the window head moves only from $\baseWindowC$ to $\baseWindowD$ between $\nseq=512$ and
$\nseq=2048$, while the global architectures keep improving, the best reaching
$\baseBestD$. Whether that plateau is the radius's or the architecture's is a question
the laboratory can answer exactly: the best estimator of any kind that sees only a
window of radius $r$ is $\E[a_i\mid x_{i-r},\dots,x_{i+r}]$, computed by sum-product on
the sub-chain (\cref{app:audit}, \cref{fig:locality}). Pooled over the schedule its error at radius $4$ is
$\audLocFloorrfourRhozeropeightfive$ (peaking at $\audLocPeakFloorRfour$ at
$t=\audLocPeakLevel$), at radius $8$ it is $\audLocFloorreightRhozeropeightfive$, and
the Gaussian twin's own windowed estimator reaches the full LMMSE by radius
$4$ ($\audLocGaussrfourRhozeropeightfive$ against $\audLocLmmseRhozeropeightfive$): what a
window beyond a few sites carries is non-Gaussian information. The window head sits at
$\baseWindowD$ against a radius-$4$ floor of $\audLocFloorrfourRhozeropeightfive$; the
radius is a ceiling only to the extent the two coincide, and the remainder is the
architecture and its training.

Given enough clean sequences the networks pass the second-order floor: $\baseBelowFloorND$
of the six sit below $\baseLmmseD$ at $\nseq=2048$ (\cref{prop:lmmse}). Nothing intrinsic
holds them at second order; they need data. The structured estimator is ahead at every size
nonetheless---$\capEmeightD$ at $\nseq=2048$, a paired factor $\baseRatioBestD$
($[\baseRatioBestDlo,\baseRatioBestDhi]$, favouring EM--BP in $\baseWinsBestD$ of
$\baseSeeds$ seeds) below the best network. That factor is an upper bound on the
advantage under interface~A: the fully connected network and the transformer select the
last rung of their $32{,}000$-step ladder in $\baseAtCapMlpD\%$ and
$\baseAtCapTransformerD\%$ of the cells at $\nseq=2048$ and the bi-GRU in
$\baseAtCapBigruD\%$, so the largest networks were still improving when training stopped.
And the comparison is not information-matched: the networks see clean sequences and a
fresh corruption at every gradient step, EM--BP one noised view per sequence and no clean
chain at all.

\paragraph{The comparison at matched information.}
Interface~C is EM--BP's own: one noised view per sequence, split across the twelve levels,
no clean chain, and---since the true score is not available for selection in that
setting---the checkpoint selected on the implicit score-matching objective of a held-out
noisy bundle. Trained by implicit score matching on exactly that bundle, the
\matchedBestArchD{} reaches $\matchedBestD$ at $\nseq=2048$ against EM--BP's
$\capEmeightD$ on the same bundles, a paired factor $\matchedRatioBestD$
($[\matchedRatioBestDlo,\matchedRatioBestDhi]$, in $\matchedWinsD$ of $\baseSeeds$ seeds;
$\matchedRatioOfMeansD$ as a ratio of means); the transformer reaches
$\matchedIsmoneviewTransformerD$. Under interface~B---the clean sequence supplied as a
regression target, but with one fixed corruption per sequence---the same head reaches
$\matchedFixedD$, a paired factor $\matchedFixedRatioD$
($[\matchedFixedRatioDlo,\matchedFixedRatioDhi]$): even given strictly more than EM--BP
sees, one corruption per sequence is not enough. The gap between the interface-A rows
and these is the value of clean data with fresh corruptions; the factor of
$\matchedRatioBestD$ that remains at equal information is what the factorisation and the
exact E-step are worth, and it is larger than the factor at the standard interface, not
smaller. EM--BP's own selection rule does not need the true score either: selecting its
iteration count by held-out one-view log-evidence instead of by validation risk against
the true kernel changes its test error by $\audSelDiffA$ at $\nseq=32$, the size at which the
choice of rung matters most (\cref{app:audit}).

\paragraph{Length extrapolation.}
\input{sections/tab-length}
Fitted at $\nsites=32$ and evaluated against the exact score of longer chains
(\cref{tab:length}), the EM--BP kernel holds $\lenEmbpDLthreetwo$ at $\nsites=32$ and
$\lenEmbpDLtwofivesix$ at $\nsites=256$: the law is local, so inference at any length is the
same exact recursion. The convolutional and recurrent networks improve slightly, as they
should---longer chains have proportionally fewer boundary sites---and the window head
improves most, since a sizeable part of its error at $\nsites=32$ is at the zero-padded
boundary. The transformer with learned relative positions does not: it degrades from
$\lenTransformerDLthreetwo$ to $\lenTransformerDLtwofivesix$, a failure of the positional
encoding beyond its training length rather than of the score it learned
\citep{press2022alibi,kazemnejad2023positional}.
