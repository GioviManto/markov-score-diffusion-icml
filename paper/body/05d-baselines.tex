\Cref{tab:baselines} compares six architectures trained under one recipe (per-example noise
level, batch $64$, Adam with clipping, both output parameterisations, checkpoint selected on
validation), each with the configuration that won its family's screen on the development
seeds (\cref{tab:screen}, \cref{app:protocol}), on the test bundles of the capacity
experiment. Three things separate them. Weight sharing separates the fully connected network
from the rest at every size ($\baseWorstA$ against $\baseBestA$ at $\nseq=32$). Locality is
the better prior when sequences are scarce---the window head is the best network at
$\nseq=32$---but it is a ceiling rather than a prior once they are not: the window head moves
only from $\baseWindowC$ to $\baseWindowD$ between $\nseq=512$ and $\nseq=2048$, while the
global architectures keep improving, the best reaching $\baseBestD$. A radius-$4$ window
cannot represent a message that has travelled the length of the chain, and the chain's exact
score is exactly such a message.

Given enough clean sequences the networks pass the second-order floor: $\baseBelowFloorND$
of the six sit below $\baseLmmseD$ at $\nseq=2048$ (\cref{prop:lmmse}). Nothing intrinsic
holds them at second order; they need data. The structured estimator is ahead at every size
nonetheless---$\capEmeightD$ at $\nseq=2048$, a paired factor $\baseRatioBestD$
($[\baseRatioBestDlo,\baseRatioBestDhi]$, favouring EM--BP in $\baseWinsBestD$ of
$\baseSeeds$ seeds) below the best network---but that comparison is not
information-matched: the networks see clean sequences and a fresh corruption at every
gradient step, EM--BP one noised view per sequence and no clean chain at all.

\paragraph{The comparison at matched information.}
Trained at EM--BP's own interface, the same architectures lose most of their advantage from
scale. The best matched arm at $\nseq=2048$ is the $\matchedBestArchD$ at
$\matchedBestD$---$\matchedBestArmD$---against EM--BP's $\capEmeightD$ on the same bundles, a
paired factor $\matchedRatioBestD$ ($[\matchedRatioBestDlo,\matchedRatioBestDhi]$). Implicit
score matching on the one-view bundle, which uses no clean target at all, is worse again
($\matchedIsmoneviewWindowD$ for the window head, $\matchedIsmoneviewTransformerD$ for the
transformer). The gap between the standard-recipe rows and these is the value of clean data
with fresh corruptions; the factor of $\matchedRatioBestD$ that remains is what the
factorisation and the exact E-step are worth at equal information.

\paragraph{Length extrapolation.}
\input{sections/tab-length}
Fitted at $\nsites=32$ and evaluated against the exact score of longer chains
(\cref{tab:length}), the EM--BP kernel holds $\lenEmbpDLthreetwo$ at $\nsites=32$ and
$\lenEmbpDLtwofivesix$ at $\nsites=256$: the law is local, so inference at any length is the
same exact recursion. The convolutional and recurrent networks improve slightly, as they
should---longer chains have proportionally fewer boundary sites. The transformer with
learned relative positions does not: it degrades from $\lenTransformerDLthreetwo$ to
$\lenTransformerDLtwofivesix$, a failure of the positional encoding beyond its training
length rather than of the score it learned \citep{press2022alibi,kazemnejad2023positional}.
