\Cref{tab:baselines} compares six architectures trained under one recipe (per-example
noise level, batch $64$, Adam with clipping, both output parameterisations, checkpoint
selected on validation), each with the configuration that won its family's screen on the
development seeds (\cref{tab:screen}, \cref{app:protocol}), on the same test bundles as
the capacity experiment. Weight sharing is what the fully connected network lacks: the
window head reaches $\baseWindowD$ against the MLP's $\baseMlpD$ at $\nseq=2048$. Global
propagation adds little at these sizes: the dilated TCN, the bidirectional recurrence,
the transformer and the U-Net reach $\baseTcnD$, $\baseBigruD$, $\baseTransformerD$ and
$\baseUnetD$. Every network sits at or above the second-order floor $\baseLmmseD$; the
structured estimator sits at $\capEmeightD$, a factor $\baseRatioWindowD$
($[\baseRatioWindowDlo,\baseRatioWindowDhi]$) below the best network at $\nseq=2048$ and
$\baseRatioWindowA$ at $\nseq=32$.

\paragraph{Information-matched training.}
Trained by implicit score matching on the one-view noisy bundle that EM--BP sees, with
the checkpoint selected on the implicit objective of a held-out noisy bundle and the
true score used only to report, the window head reaches
$\matchedIsmoneviewWindowD$ and the transformer $\matchedIsmoneviewTransformerD$ at
$\nseq=2048$; with clean targets but a single fixed corruption per sequence they reach
$\matchedDsmonecorruptionWindowD$ and $\matchedDsmonecorruptionTransformerD$. The
distance between these and the standard-recipe rows is the value of the clean signal
and of fresh corruptions; the distance to EM--BP at the same interface is the value of
the structure.
