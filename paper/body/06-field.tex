\section{A physical benchmark: the one-dimensional $\phi^4$ field}
\label{sec:field}

The linear chain tests the method on a model chosen for solvability. The $\phi^4$ field of
\cref{sec:setup} tests it on a model chosen by physics: spatial snapshots of the stochastic
Allen--Cahn equation, whose stationary law is a Gibbs measure with domains, kinks and a tunable
correlation length. Because its lattice snapshots are exactly Markov (\cref{eq:tilted}) and its
transfer operator can be diagonalised, the exact score, the exact samples and the exact physical
statistics are all available, so every estimator can be compared with the truth and with a physical
reading. The comparison follows the four arms of the proposal that motivated it: a learned pair
energy, Gaussian closure, a window CNN, and the exact oracle.

\paragraph{Cells.}
Two knobs set the chain, the temperature $\Temp$ and the lattice spacing $\latt$, and we run six cells
(\cref{tab:fieldcells}). The base cell ($\Temp=0.3$, $\latt=1$, $\nsites=32$) is cold enough for domains.
Three hotter cells hold the correlation length in sites fixed by solving for $\latt$ at each $\Temp$, so
they compare the field with the linear-chain laboratory at matched second-order structure. Two refinements
of the base cell hold the physical length fixed ($\latt=0.5$ with $64$ sites, $\latt=0.25$ with $128$), so the
correlation length in sites grows like $1/\latt$ (from $\fldXiHone$ to $\fldXiHzeroptwofive$). The linear
floor of every cell is the risk of the best linear (Gaussian) estimator (\cref{prop:lmmse}); it is large
where the field is cold and nonlinear.

\paragraph{Temperature and domains.}
At fixed spacing the physics is monotone in the temperature (\cref{fig:fieldsweep}): lowering $\Temp$
lengthens domains (the correlation length in sites grows from $\fldXiHot$ at $\Temp=2$ to $\fldXiCold$ at $\Temp=0.2$,
exponentially in $1/\Temp$ with a slope that approaches the kink energy $E_{\mathrm{kink}}=2\sqrt2/3$ as
the field cools; \cref{app:field}), sharpens the bimodality
(coefficient from $\fldBimodHot$ to $\fldBimodCold$), and makes the linear floor higher
($\fldFloorFixedHHot$ at $\Temp=2$ to $\fldFloorFixedHCold$ at $\Temp=0.2$). The raw count of sign changes
per unit length is not a physical kink density (a rough field crosses zero inside a domain, and more finely on
finer lattices), so we count kinks after merging runs shorter than one kink width, which is
lattice-independent (\cref{app:field}). There is no transition: the crossover is smooth.

\paragraph{Arms and protocol.}
Every estimator learns from noised sequences only. EM--BP with five kernel families
(\cref{sec:learning}): the paper's mixture ($25$ numbers), a spline tilt ($25$), a Legendre tilt ($7$),
the quartic site energy ($5$), and an MDN ($1\,516$); a window CNN and a transformer, each trained under two
recipes, implicit score matching on one noised view per sequence (ISM, EM--BP's own interface) and denoising
score matching on clean targets with one fixed corruption (DSM, which gives the network strictly more); the
linear floor; and the exact oracle. Sizes are $\nseq\in\{32,128,512,2048\}$ for the kernels and $\{128,2048\}$
for the networks, sixteen seeds, the same twelve levels, and the held-out risk against the exact score selects
EM's stopping point for every arm alike.

\paragraph{The site energy wins on every cell; the paper's kernel does not leave the linear floor.}
\Cref{tab:fieldscore} is the central result. At $\nseq=2048$ the five-parameter site energy reaches
$\fldSiteFloorMin$--$\fldSiteFloorMax$ of the linear floor across the six cells, the best method on every
cell at every size on average, and $\fldSiteOverSplineMin$--$\fldSiteOverSplineMax$ of the $25$-parameter spline's
risk; the spline is next, and the networks, whichever recipe, are above the linear floor on every cell with
$\Temp\ge0.5$ and at least $\fldNetOverSplineMin$ times the spline's risk on every cell at both network sizes. The paper's own mixture
kernel is the instructive failure: it sits on the linear floor ($\fldMixFloorMin$--$\fldMixFloorMax$ times it
at $\nseq=2048$) on every cell, because its expected next value is a \emph{linear} function of the current one,
whereas the field's is not (\cref{app:field}); with more data its gap to the spline widens rather than closes (its risk is
$\fldMixSplineBaseA$ times the spline's at $\nseq=512$ and $\fldMixSplineBaseB$ times at $2048$ on the
base cell). A kernel whose family contains the
physics pays nothing for it, a kernel whose family misses it never recovers; the choice of kernel family
is the modelling decision, and for a gradient flow the right family has five numbers.

\paragraph{The fitted kernel reads as physics.}
The site energy is the physicist's pair energy, so its parameters are physical. Fitted through noise to
$\nseq=2048$ sequences, the temperature is recovered to within $10\%$ in $\fldThetaOkFits$ of $\fldThetaFits$ fits
and the barrier height to within $20\%$ in $\fldBarrierOkFits$ of $\fldBarrierFits$ fits on the five cells where the
registered reading asks for it (\cref{tab:fieldpair}); at $\Temp=2$, where the barrier is small against the thermal energy, its
error falls like $\nseq^{-\fldBarrierSlope}$ and every one of sixteen seeds is within $20\%$ at
$\nseq=\fldBarrierBigM$ (\cref{app:field}), so it is a sample-size limit and not a floor.

\paragraph{Generated fields are physically right.}
A score can be accurate and still generate wrong fields, so we sample from each fitted kernel by one forward
sweep and compare physical statistics with exact draws (\cref{tab:fieldsamples}). Samples from the site energy
and the spline reproduce the coarse kink density within $\fldKinkDevSite$ and $\fldKinkDevSpline$ of the
truth on every cell, the mean domain length likewise, and the whole distribution of domain lengths to
$\fldDomQuantDevSite$ and $\fldDomQuantDevSpline$ at every quantile, statistically indistinguishable from exact
samples. The mixture's samples have $\fldMixKinkColdMin$--$\fldMixKinkColdMax$ times too many kinks on the cold
cells and domains $\fldMixDomShortMin$--$\fldMixDomShortMax$ too short at the median: a linear drift cannot hold a
domain, which is the smearing of the Gaussian closure seen in the samples.

\paragraph{Refinement: what a fixed receptive field costs.}
Because the correlation length in sites grows like $1/\latt$, a denoiser with a fixed window should lose
ground as the lattice is refined and BP should not, which is the proposal's sharp prediction. The window CNN's
radius is screened per cell ($2$, $4$ and $8$ sites at $\latt=1,\tfrac12,\tfrac14$, a constant physical
reach, the last at the largest radius offered). Its absolute risk falls
($\fldWindowRiskHone$, $\fldWindowRiskHzeroptwofive$ at $\latt=1$, $0.25$), but the structured kernel's falls
faster: the window's risk relative to the spline grows from $\fldWindowSplineHone$ to $\fldWindowSplineHzeroptwofive$
and relative to the linear floor from $\fldWindowFloorHone$ to $\fldWindowFloorHzeroptwofive$, so on the finest
lattice the window is worse than the Gaussian closure. \Cref{fig:fieldruntime} puts this beside cost: the networks
own the cheap end of the runtime--accuracy frontier ($\fldNetCostMin$--$\fldNetCostMax$\,ms per sequence against
$\fldBpCostLone$--$\fldBpCostLthree$\,ms for grid BP, which is shared by every BP-based arm) and the structured kernels
the accurate end, with nothing between them.

\paragraph{Speciation: when does the denoiser commit to a domain?}
The proposal that motivated this benchmark asks to watch the denoiser commit to whole domains as the noise is
removed. For a noised sequence the exact posterior gives each site $p_i=P(a_i>0\mid\vx)$, and its commitment is
$|2p_i-1|$, zero when the two wells are equally likely and one when the site has chosen. Averaged over sites, the
commitment falls from one at $t=0$ to zero at high noise; we call $t_c$ the level where it crosses one half and
compare it with the \emph{Gaussian twin}, whose commitment is available in closed form (\cref{app:field}). The
posterior is calibrated, so the commitment is the probability of guessing the sign right (the two agree to
$\fldSpecCalibDev$ on average over levels). \Cref{fig:speciation} gives the three readings. Colder fields commit
earlier in the reverse process: $t_c$ falls from $\fldSpecTcCold$ at $\Temp=0.2$ to $\fldSpecTcHot$ at $\Temp=2$. The
exact posterior commits at higher noise than its Gaussian twin at every temperature, by $\fldSpecExcessCold$ in $t$
when the field is coldest and $\fldSpecExcessHot$ when it is hottest: at $\Temp=0.2$ the exact posterior is
half-committed when the noise variance is $\fldSpecNoiseRatioExact$ times the signal's, its twin only at
$\fldSpecNoiseRatioTwin$ times, so it is the two humps and not only the correlations that decide when the
denoiser commits. At fixed $\Temp=0.2$ the commitment time of the central sites grows with the chain length
($\fldSpecTcLfour$ at $\nsites=4$ to $\fldSpecTcLsixtyfour$ at $64$) and stops growing once the chain is several
correlation lengths long ($\fldSpecTcLonetwentyeight$ and $\fldSpecTcLtwofiftysix$ at $128$ and $256$); below the
correlation length it moves one-for-one with $\tfrac12\ln(1+\lambda_{\max})$, $\lambda_{\max}$ the largest eigenvalue of
the covariance (slope $\fldSpecSlope$), the finite-chain analogue of the speciation time of
\citet{biroli2024dynamical}. A second reading checks the lattice against the continuum: as $\latt\to0$ the physical
correlation length converges (it changes by $\fldKinkConvergence$ from $\latt=1/16$ to $1/32$), and the dilute-kink law
$\corrlen\propto\Temp^{1/2}e^{E_{\mathrm{kink}}/\Temp}$ is approached as $\Temp$ falls but not reached on $\Temp\in[0.1,0.3]$: a
four-parameter fit returns a barrier of $\fldKinkFit$ against $2\sqrt2/3$ (a registered prediction, within $2\%$, failed),
and we do not claim the asymptotic constant.

\paragraph{Beyond the score.}
The same fitted kernels serve other tasks without refitting (\cref{app:field}). Hiding sites of a noised
sequence and drawing posterior sequences by forward-filter backward-sample, the learned site energy and spline
match the oracle within $\fldInpaintOracleDev$ in error and width and cover a nominal $90\%$ interval
$\fldInpaintCovMin$--$\fldInpaintCovMax$ of the time, while on the base cell a Gaussian smoother with the true
covariance is only $\fldInpaintLinErrMin$--$\fldInpaintLinErrMax$ worse in error, with
$\fldInpaintLinWidthMin$--$\fldInpaintLinWidthMax$ wider intervals. On snapshots taken during coarsening after a quench, where the law is not Gibbs and the reference is
therefore the clean snapshot (so a perfect denoiser does not reach zero and the ratios are compressed), the
site energy still beats an empirical Gaussian smoother at every time (risk ratio $\fldQuenchEarly$ shortly after
the quench, $\fldQuenchEq$ at equilibrium), and the flexible spline adds nothing.
