\section{Where the structure stops}
\label{sec:limits}

The structured estimator assumes four things: that the data are Markov, that the kernel family is wide enough to
contain the truth, that the grid resolves the kernel, and that the law is stationary. Each can fail, and each failure
has a signature we can measure.

\subsection{The kernel family: when does a flexible kernel pay?}
\label{sec:kernelclass}

The simple kernels are exactly specified on the two chains above, so the question is what happens when they are not.
Two experiments take the truth out of the family, and one asks the neural kernel the same question.

\paragraph{An unrestricted kernel on the field.}
On the $\phi^4$ field the 100-parameter unrestricted kernel, which assumes no Gaussian bond, costs
$\limUnrestrictedPriceMin$--$\limUnrestrictedPriceMax$ times the site energy's risk, and the price \emph{grows}
with the data rather than shrinking ($\limUnrestrictedGrowthA$ at $\nseq=128$ to $\limUnrestrictedGrowthB$ at $2048$ on the
base cell): the exactly specified model improves roughly like the sample size and the unrestricted one more slowly.
Generality is paid for where it is not needed.

\paragraph{A chain outside the family.}
Replace the Gaussian bond by a sharp Laplace bond: the pair energy $|a'-a|$ in place of $(a'-a)^2$ on the same double well, with the bond
scale and the well depth calibrated beforehand to the base cell's lag-one correlation and kurtosis (\cref{app:field}).
This chain is outside the tilted-walk class, so the site energy and the spline
are misspecified; its exact samples and oracle are available from the grid. The site energy and the spline
stall at a risk of about $\limLapStall$ while the unrestricted kernel keeps improving, overtakes them between
$\nseq=128$ and $512$, and ends $\limLapUnrestrictedGain\times$ below the site energy at $2048$ in every one of
eight seeds. A neural kernel does better still: the MDN reaches $\limLapMdn$ at $\nseq=2048$ against
$\limLapUnrestricted$ for the unrestricted kernel and $\limLapSite$ for the site energy, below the site energy
in $\limLapMdnBelowSite$ of eight seeds and, against a registered prediction that parsimony would win, below
the unrestricted kernel in $\limLapMdnBelowUnr$ (its fits were still improving at the last checkpoint in
$\limLapMdnAtCap$ of eight, so its risk is an upper bound).

\paragraph{Subsampling the field.}
Observing a fine lattice only at every $s$-th site gives a chain that is still exactly Markov, with kernel $\kernel^s$,
but no longer a tilted walk. The coarse-grained physics is unchanged across strides
(kink density and domain length agree within $\limStrideAgree$), but the five- and twenty-five-parameter
kernels are now misspecified: their risk at stride $4$ is $\limStrideGrowthSite$ and $\limStrideGrowthSpline$ times its
stride-one value (kernels fitted on clean chains, sixteen seeds), though it stays far below the linear floor
($\limStrideSiteRisk$ and $\limStrideSplineRisk$ against $\limStrideFloor$). Here the network kernel earns its parameters:
with its ladder extended to $16\,000$ updates the MDN is below the site energy at stride $4$ in $\limStrideMdnBelow$ of sixteen seeds
at $\limStrideMdnRatio$ times its risk, while at stride $1$, where the site energy is exactly specified, the site energy is
$\limStrideOneRatio$ times better. The MDN was still improving at its last checkpoint in $\limStrideMdnAtCap$ of the
sixteen fits, so its risks are upper bounds and the margin can only widen.

The rule that emerges is simple. A kernel with a few parameters that contains the physics is better than a flexible one,
the paper's own linear-drift mixture is correct for a linear chain and wrong for a double well, a network kernel loses to it
on the linear chain by a factor $\limMdnLinearLo$--$\limMdnLinearHi$ and also to the best plain score network, and the flexible
kernels (unrestricted, neural) are the right tools exactly when the simple family is wrong. The unrestricted kernel is therefore
best used as a misspecification \emph{test}: if it beats the family it is compared with at large $\nseq$, the family's
assumption is wrong.

\subsection{The Markov assumption: a latent shared by the whole sequence}
\label{sec:latent}

The estimator is a model of $p(a_{i+1}\mid a_i)$ only. If a hidden variable is shared by a whole sequence, the observed chain is no longer
Markov, a bias remains that more data cannot remove, and its size is computable. We start with a closed-form case on the linear chain:
$X_i=Gb+U_i$ with $G=\pm1$ a fair sign per sequence and $U$ the Gaussian linear chain, whose exact score is a mixture of two Gaussian
chains. The first-order kernel then stops improving: at $b=2$ four times more data change its risk by $\limLatentFlat$, and it ends
$\limLatentWorseMin$--$\limLatentWorseMax$ times \emph{worse} than the linear floor in every one of sixteen seeds. The repair is the same mixture
kernel on the augmented state $(X_i,G)$, the two phases tied by reflection, with the same number of parameters: BP runs on pairs, inferring each
sequence's sign, and the risk keeps falling with the data ($\limLatentAugFall$ over the same factor of four), ending $\limLatentFloorRatio$ times below the
linear floor and $\limLatentFirstOverAug$ times below the first-order kernel at $b=2$ (\cref{fig:repair}). A surprise is instructive: the repaired model
also helps when there is no latent ($b=0$). We traced it to the reflection symmetry of the data: fitting the plain kernel on each sequence together with its
sign-flipped copy is $\limSymGainMin$--$\limSymGainMax$ better than the plain fit, and in average risk it is worth as much as fitting on twice as many sequences.
The same mechanism for general departures from Markov dependence is measured in \cref{fig:misspec}.

\subsection{Resolution, discretisation and what we do not claim}
\label{sec:notclaim}

A flexible kernel can need a finer grid than the one the simple kernels use (\cref{sec:compute}), and the lattice itself is a modelling choice:
the correlation length in sites grows like $1/\latt$, so a fixed-window network falls behind under refinement (\cref{sec:field}) and the
advantage of structure grows. We also do not claim the following.
\emph{Superiority over networks in general:} the comparison equalises the number of sequences under each method's standard recipe and,
separately, matches the information interface with no oracle on either side; it does not rank learning algorithms, and the networks
timed in \cref{sec:compute} are the largest variants.
\emph{Exactness beyond quadrature:} grid BP is exact for the discretised model and accurate to the measured quadrature, and we
say which where it matters.
\emph{A Kalman filter:} the Gaussian pass is a smoother.
\emph{A phase transition:} the field's low-temperature crossover is smooth in one dimension.
\emph{A Laplace closure:} the transition-family message ansatz is not exact beyond the first site.
\emph{A general SPDE transition:} a fixed driving-noise family such as the mixture kernel is a different object from the field's tilted walk, and
it is misspecified there through its drift.
