Diffusion models are trained to approximate the score of a noised data distribution,
a target that is unknown for almost every distribution of interest. We study a family
in which it is known exactly and non-trivial: a first-order Markov chain with
continuous, non-Gaussian transitions under coordinatewise Ornstein--Uhlenbeck noise,
where conditioning leaves the posterior factor graph a tree and one forward--backward
sweep of functional message passing returns the exact score. This laboratory lets us
separate what diffusion noise does to different orders of structure. We prove that the
channel attenuates every Hermite mode of degree $m$ by $e^{-mt}$, so that the Fisher
information one noised sequence carries about an order-$m$ feature decays as
$e^{-2mt}$: correlation (degree two) survives as $e^{-4t}$, non-Gaussian shape (degree
four) as $e^{-8t}$, and the gap between the true score and that of the covariance-matched
Gaussian---which we show is exactly what Gaussian message closure computes---is
$\tfrac16\lVert\kappa_4\rVert_F^2e^{-8t}$, with the crossover set by the covariance
spectrum. Tree-exact inference verifies the rates and the constants. We then learn the
transition law from noised sequences alone, by expectation--maximisation around the
same recursion with Fisher's identity supplying the exact gradient, and show that it
recovers fourth-order structure from strictly less information than the denoisers it is
compared against: one noised view per sequence, never a clean chain, against clean targets
with a fresh corruption at every gradient step. \needsdata{final baseline, generation and
