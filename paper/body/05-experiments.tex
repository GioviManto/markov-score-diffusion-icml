\section{What explicit structure buys, and where it stops}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section answers the third question in three steps: what explicit structure buys
and why (capacity), whether the advantage is the structure or the data the estimator is
given (three information interfaces, and a comparison at EM--BP's own), and where it stops
(length, generation, misspecification). All comparisons use the frozen configuration of
\cref{app:protocol}: Laplace chain, $\corr=0.85$, $\nsites=32$, twelve log-spaced
levels on $[0.05,3]$, and the schedule-pooled relative score error against the tree-exact
reference. Paired seeds are the inferential unit, sixteen at the frozen configuration and
eight in the sweeps across regimes; every selection is made on development seeds or on a
validation bundle that never enters a reported number. Three information interfaces are
distinguished throughout: (A) the standard denoising recipe, clean sequences with a fresh
corruption at every step; (B) clean sequences with one fixed corruption each; (C) one noised
view per sequence and nothing else, which is what EM--BP sees. Three reference points
bracket every estimator (\cref{app:oracles}): the isotropic score $-\vx$ at
$\isoFloorLaplace$, the LMMSE oracle with the true covariance at $\lmmseFloorLaplace$,
the floor of every second-order estimator by \cref{prop:lmmse}, and the exact score at
zero.
