\Cref{tab:capacity} places every arm on identical test bundles. A Gaussian-innovation
kernel ($\ncomp=1$) lands on the LMMSE floor at every size
($\capEmoneA$, $\capEmoneB$, $\capEmoneC$, $\capEmoneD$ against the floor
$\capLmmseA$--$\capLmmseD$), as \cref{prop:lmmse} requires; the mixture kernel goes
far below it, reaching $\capEmeightD$ at $\nseq=2048$, $\capFloorRatioD\times$ under the
floor. The paired $\ncomp=8$ versus $\ncomp=1$ difference is
$\capDiffD$ (bootstrap $[\capDiffDlo,\capDiffDhi]$) at $\nseq=2048$ and
$\capDiffA$ ($[\capDiffAlo,\capDiffAhi]$) already at $\nseq=32$, in favour of the
mixture in $\capWinsD$ and $\capWinsA$ of $\capSeeds$ seeds. At $\nseq=2048$ the
$\ncomp=8$ arm still selects the top of its $1200$-iteration ladder in $\capAtCapeightD$ of
$\capSeeds$ seeds, so its error there is an upper bound on what a longer ladder would give. The fitted innovation
excess kurtosis is $\capKurteightD$ at $\nseq=2048$ against the true $3$; $\capUnresolved$
of $\capCells$ fitted cells fail the two-grid-cell resolution gate and are reported, not
dropped. The last row of \cref{tab:capacity} is the family's infinite-data reference, the
$\ncomp$-component mixture closest in Kullback--Leibler divergence to the Laplace
innovation: its $\ncomp=1$ member scores $\capPopone$, the LMMSE floor to three decimals,
and at $\ncomp=8$ it scores $\capPopeight$, so the fitted kernel at $\nseq=2048$ sits
$\capPopRatioeightD\times$ above what its own family can express, a data limit and not a
capacity limit (\cref{app:capacity}).
