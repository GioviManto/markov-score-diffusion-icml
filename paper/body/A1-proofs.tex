\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{\Cref{prop:lmmse}}
Write $\lmsg^{\mathrm{p}}_i$ for the forward message at site $i$ after projection and
$\ell_i$ for the Gaussian unary likelihood. We show by induction that every projected
message equals the corresponding exact message of the covariance-matched Gaussian chain.
The initial message is Gaussian or projected to the Gaussian with the same first two
moments, which is the Gaussian chain's initial message. If $\lmsg^{\mathrm{p}}_{i-1}$ is
that chain's message, with mean $m$ and variance $v$, multiplying by $\ell_{i-1}$ gives
a Gaussian with the precision-weighted moments, identical in both chains; the transition
integral against $a_i=\corr a_{i-1}+\varepsilon_i$ is the density of $\corr a_{i-1}+\varepsilon_i$,
whose mean and variance are $(\corr m',\corr^2v'+\ivar)$ for every innovation with
moments $(0,\ivar)$, so $\Pgauss$ returns the Gaussian chain's next message. The
backward message is $h(\corr a)$ with $h$ the density of $a''-\varepsilon$ under the
incoming Gaussian, of mean $m'$ and variance $v'+\ivar$; as a function of $a$ its
normalised moments are $(m'/\corr,(v'+\ivar)/\corr^2)$, which is where $\corr\neq0$ is
used, starting from the terminal likelihood $\ell_{\nsites}$ (the flat terminal message
is not normalisable and contributes no factor). Every projected belief is therefore the
Gaussian chain's belief, whose posterior mean is linear in $\vx$ and coincides with the
LMMSE estimator $e^{-t}\Sig_0\Sig_t^{-1}\vx$. Since the recursion never read $\innov$
beyond $(0,\ivar)$, the estimator is the same for every such law; nothing assumes
stationarity, since the induction carries site-dependent moments. The algebraic steps
(the two moment maps as integrals, the covariance identities, and the optimality of the
normal-equation gain in arbitrary dimension) are machine-checked in Lean~4
(\codefile{lean/README.md}); the projection step itself is not. The two consequences
stated after the proposition follow from the same fact: sum-product with a Gaussian
kernel is the Gaussian chain's smoother with that kernel's covariance, a linear map, and
the LMMSE of the true covariance is the best linear map in mean square; and the Gaussian
log-likelihood of a noised sample is a function of its sample covariance, whose
expectation $e^{-2t}\Sig_0+\Dt\Id$ identifies $(\corr,\ivar)$.

\subsection{\Cref{thm:hermite}}
For $z\sim\normal{0}{1}$ and real $a$, $\alpha$ with $\beta=\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}$, the
generating function $e^{sx-s^2/2}=\sum_n\Hen{n}(x)s^n/n!$ evaluated at
$x=\alpha a+\beta z$ and averaged over $z$ gives
$e^{s\alpha a-s^2\alpha^2/2}=\sum_n\Hen{n}(a)(\alpha s)^n/n!$, hence
$\E_z\Hen{n}(\alpha a+\beta z)=\alpha^n\Hen{n}(a)$. The coordinates of $\vz$ are
independent given $\va$, so the product form holds for $H_\nu$; integrating over
$\va\sim\Pz$ requires only $\E|\va|^{|\nu|}<\infty$. The cases $n\le4$ are verified in
Lean as polynomial identities under the first four Gaussian moments.

\subsection{\Cref{thm:decay,thm:general}}
\label{app:proof-decay}

\paragraph{Exact identities.}
With $\alpha=e^{-t}$ and $\Dt=1-\alpha^2$, Bayes' rule gives
$\Pt(\vx)=\gamma_{\Dt}(\vx)\,\E_{\Pz}\exp\bigl(\alpha\vx\cdot\va/\Dt-\alpha^2|\va|^2/2\Dt\bigr)$,
where $\gamma_{\Dt}$ is the $\normal{0}{\Dt\Id}$ density. Hence
$\log\Pt(\vx)=\log\gamma_{\Dt}(\vx)+\Lambda(s,b)$ with $s=\alpha\vx/\Dt$, $b=\alpha^2/\Dt$
and $\Lambda(s,b)=\log\E_{\Pz}\exp(s\cdot\va-b|\va|^2/2)$;
$\E[\va\mid\vx]=\nabla_s\Lambda$ and $\score=-\vx/\Dt+(\alpha/\Dt)\nabla_s\Lambda$.

\paragraph{Where the expansion lives.}
Under \cref{ass:exp} the integrand is dominated by $e^{\lambda|\va|}$ on
$\{|s|\le\lambda\}\times\{b\ge0\}$, so $\Lambda$ is finite there and real-analytic in $s$
on $|s|<\lambda$ for every $b\ge0$, with Taylor coefficients at the origin equal to the
joint cumulants of the linear form $s\cdot\va$ and the quadratic form $|\va|^2/2$; the
tilted laws $\propto\Pz(\va)e^{\theta(s\cdot\va-b|\va|^2/2)}$, $\theta\in[0,1]$, have
moments of every order bounded uniformly on $\{|s|\le\lambda/2,\,0\le b\le1\}$ by
constants depending on $\lambda$ and $\E e^{\lambda|\va|}$. Without \cref{ass:exp} the
expansion at $b=0$ is not available: $\Lambda(s,0)=\log\E e^{s\cdot\va}$ is infinite for
every $s\neq0$ when $\Pz$ has only finitely many moments.

\paragraph{Grading.}
Expand $\Lambda$ in $(s,b)$ at the origin and grade a monomial $s^{j}b^{k}$ by $j+2k$;
since $s=O(\alpha)$ and $b=O(\alpha^2)$, the grade is the order in $\alpha$. By the
symmetry of $\Pz$ under $\va\mapsto-\va$ only even grades occur:
$\Lambda=\Lambda_2+\Lambda_4+R_6$, with
$\Lambda_2=\tfrac12s^{\!\top}\Sig_0s-\tfrac b2\operatorname{tr}\Sig_0$ and
\[
  \Lambda_4=\tfrac1{24}\kfour[s,s,s,s]
   -\tfrac b4\sum_i\operatorname{cum}(s\!\cdot\!\va,s\!\cdot\!\va,a_i^2)
   +\tfrac{b^2}8\sum_{ij}\operatorname{cum}(a_i^2,a_j^2),
\]
where $\operatorname{cum}$ is the joint cumulant under $\Pz$ and the squares enter because
$b$ multiplies $|\va|^2/2$: $\operatorname{cum}(s\!\cdot\!\va,s\!\cdot\!\va,a_i^2)
=\operatorname{cum}(s\!\cdot\!\va,s\!\cdot\!\va,a_i,a_i)+2(\Sig_0s)_i^2$ and
$\operatorname{cum}(a_i^2,a_j^2)=\kfour[i,i,j,j]+2(\Sig_0)_{ij}^2$, the added terms being
functions of $\Sig_0$ alone. Every fourth-order joint cumulant of linear forms in $\va$
equals its value under the covariance twin plus a linear contraction of $\kfour$, so
$\Lambda_4-\Lambda_4^{\mathrm{G}}$ is linear in $\kfour$ and
$\Lambda_2=\Lambda_2^{\mathrm{G}}$. The remainder $R_6$ is the Lagrange
remainder of grade six, a combination of sixth-order derivatives of $\Lambda$ at an
intermediate point of the segment to the origin, i.e.\ of tilted joint cumulants of
order six; on the bulk $B=\{|s|\le\lambda/2\}=\{|\vx|\le\lambda\Dt/2\alpha\}$ it is
bounded by $C\alpha^6(1+|\vx|)^6$ with $C$ depending only on $\lambda$ and
$\E e^{\lambda|\va|}$, by the uniform moment bound above.

\paragraph{Leading term of the score gap.}
$\score-\Sgauss=(\alpha/\Dt)\nabla_s(\Lambda-\Lambda^{\mathrm{G}})
=(\alpha/\Dt)\bigl[\nabla_s(\Lambda_4-\Lambda_4^{\mathrm{G}})+\nabla_sR_6-\nabla_sR_6^{\mathrm{G}}\bigr]$.
Substituting $s=\alpha\vx/\Dt$, $b=\alpha^2/\Dt$ and expanding in $\alpha$, the
grade-four term is
$\alpha^4\,\nabla_{\vx}\bigl[\tfrac1{24}\sum_{ijkl}\kfour[ijkl]\,{:}x_ix_jx_kx_l{:}\bigr]+O(\alpha^6(1+|\vx|)^5)$,
where ${:}\cdot{:}$ is the Wick (Hermite) product: the three contributions of $\Lambda_4$
combine into the Hermite form because $b=\alpha^2(1+O(\alpha^2))$ and the cumulant
contractions with $\delta_{ii}$ are exactly the terms that convert monomials into Hermite
polynomials. The $m$-th component is $\alpha^4\,\tfrac16\sum_{jkl}\kfour[mjkl]\,{:}x_jx_kx_l{:}$.

\paragraph{Second moment.}
Under $\gamma$, Wick's theorem gives
$\E_\gamma[{:}x_jx_kx_l{:}\,{:}x_{j'}x_{k'}x_{l'}{:}]$ equal to the number of perfect
matchings of $\{j,k,l\}$ with $\{j',k',l'\}$ respecting indices; contracted with the
symmetric tensor this yields
$\E_\gamma\bigl[(\tfrac16\sum_{jkl}\kfour[mjkl]{:}x_jx_kx_l{:})^2\bigr]
=\tfrac{3!}{36}\sum_{jkl}\kfour[mjkl]^2$, and summing over $m$,
$\E_\gamma\lVert\text{leading term}\rVert^2=\tfrac16\Frob{\kfour}^2\alpha^8$. Replacing
$\E_\gamma$ by $\E_{\Pt}$ on the bulk costs a factor $1+O(\alpha^2)$:
$\Pt/\gamma=1+\alpha^2\tfrac12\langle\Sig_0-\Id,{:}\vx\vx^{\!\top}{:}\rangle+O(\alpha^4(1+|\vx|)^4)$
there, and the degree-2 correction has carries an explicit factor $\alpha^2$, so its contribution to the second moment is of relative size $\alpha^2$. The cross term between the
leading term and the grade-six remainder is $O(\alpha^{10})$ by Cauchy--Schwarz and the
moment bound. The constant in $O(\alpha^2)$ depends on $\Pz$ and $\nsites$; for the
Gaussian chain it is $\lmax(\Sig_0)$ (last paragraph).

\paragraph{Tail.}
On $B^c=\{|\alpha\vx|>\lambda\Dt/2\}$ the expansion is not used. By the tower property
and Jensen, $\E_{\Pt}[\lVert\E[\va\mid\vx]\rVert^2\mathbf 1_{B^c}]\le\E[|\va|^2\mathbf 1_{B^c}]$,
and $B^c\subset\{\alpha|\va|>c/\alpha\}\cup\{\sqrt{\Dt}|\vz|>c/\alpha\}$ with
$c=\lambda\Dt/4$, so $\Pt(B^c)\le e^{-\lambda c/\alpha^2}\E e^{\lambda|\va|}+e^{-c^2/2\alpha^2}$,
which is $\exp(-c'e^{2t})$ under \cref{ass:exp}. Since
$\lVert\score-\Sgauss\rVert^2\le C(|\vx|^2+\alpha^2\lVert\E[\va\mid\vx]\rVert^2+\alpha^2\lVert\E^{\mathrm G}[\va\mid\vx]\rVert^2)/\Dt^2$,
Cauchy--Schwarz against $\Pt(B^c)^{1/2}$ and the finiteness of $\E|\va|^4$ make the tail
contribution below every power of $\alpha$. Together,
$\E_{\Pt}\lVert\score-\Sgauss\rVert^2=\tfrac16\Frob{\kfour}^2e^{-8t}(1+O(e^{-2t}))$,
which is \cref{eq:scoregap}. Without \cref{ass:exp} the tail probability is polynomial
in $\alpha$ (of order $\alpha^{2\nu}$ for a tail index $\nu$), and for $\nu\le8$ the
tail's own contribution is of order $\alpha^\nu$ and leads; this is the heavy-tailed
regime of \cref{sec:theory}.

\paragraph{Information and the general order.}
$\partial_\theta\log\Pt=\partial_\theta\Lambda$. If $\partial_\theta\Sig_0\neq0$ the
grade-two term $\tfrac{\alpha^2}2\langle\partial_\theta\Sig_0,{:}\vx\vx^{\!\top}{:}\rangle$
leads, and $\E_\gamma$ of its square is
$\tfrac{\alpha^4}4\cdot2\Frob{\partial_\theta\Sig_0}^2$ by Wick, giving \cref{eq:info2}.
If $\partial_\theta\Sig_0=0$ the grade-four term
$\tfrac{\alpha^4}{24}\sum\partial_\theta\kfour[ijkl]{:}x_ix_jx_kx_l{:}$ leads, with
$\gamma$-second moment $\tfrac{4!}{24^2}\Frob{\partial_\theta\kfour}^2\alpha^8$, giving
\cref{eq:info4}; the corrections and the tail are handled as above. For
\cref{thm:general}, the graded expansion of $\Lambda$ and the expansion
$\log\bigl(1+\sum_{|\nu|\ge2}\alpha^{|\nu|}\E_{\Pz}[H_\nu]H_\nu(\vx)/\nu!\bigr)$ obtained
from Mehler's kernel are two formal series in $\alpha$ for the same function whose
coefficients at each grade are polynomials in the moments of $\Pz$ up to that grade; they
agree on every compactly supported $\Pz$, where both converge, hence identically. The
grade-$m$ coefficient of $\partial_\theta\log(\Pt/\gamma)$ is therefore
$\alpha^m\sum_{|\nu|=m}\partial_\theta\E[H_\nu]H_\nu(\vx)/\nu!$ plus $\partial_\theta$ of
products of lower-grade terms, each of which vanishes by the minimality of $m$; its
$\gamma$-second moment is $\alpha^{2m}\sum_{|\nu|=m}(\partial_\theta\E H_\nu)^2/\nu!$ by
orthogonality, and the bulk correction and the tail are as before. Since
${:}\vx\vx^{\!\top}{:}$ and the degree-four Hermite polynomials are $\gamma$-orthogonal,
the leading cross-information between a degree-2 and a degree-4 coordinate vanishes, so
the leading term of the efficient information of the degree-4 coordinate after
degree-2 nuisances equals that of its raw information. For the Gaussian chain
$\vx_t\sim\normal{0}{\Sig_t(\theta)}$ exactly and the stated trace formula is the
Gaussian Fisher information; its limit $e^{4t}I_t\to\tfrac12\Frob{\partial_\theta\Sig_0}^2$
follows from $\Sig_t\to\Id$ and $\partial_\theta\Sig_t=\alpha^2\partial_\theta\Sig_0$, and
is machine-checked in Lean (\codefile{lean/README.md}, target~3).

\paragraph{\Cref{cor:samples}.}
At fixed $t>0$ the family $\{\Pt^\theta\}$ is differentiable in quadratic mean with
finite, non-singular information (smoothness of $\Lambda$ in $\theta$ under
\cref{ass:exp}), so the local asymptotic minimax theorem of H\'ajek and Le Cam applies
and the maximum-likelihood estimator is asymptotically efficient; the stated bound is
$1/I_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ with the efficient information of \cref{thm:general}.

\paragraph{The crossover.}
For the Gaussian chain the expansion of $\Pt/\gamma$ is an expansion in $\alpha^2\Sig_0$,
i.e.\ in $\alpha^2\lmax(\Sig_0)$ in operator norm, with $\lmax\le(1+\corr)/(1-\corr)$ by
the Toeplitz bound $\lmax\le\sum_k\corr^{|k|}$; $\Sig_t^{-1}$ is within $\alpha^2\lmax$
of $\Id$ in operator norm, which is the source of $\epsilon_t=O(e^{-2t}\lmax)$ and of
$\tstar=\tfrac12\log\lmax$. The same chain has $\Pt/\gamma\in L^2(\gamma)$ iff
$2\Id-\Sig_t\succ0$, i.e.\ iff $e^{2t}>\lmax-1$, so the Hermite expansion of $\Pt/\gamma$
converges in $L^2(\gamma)$ exactly above that time; for the Laplace chain it never does,
which is why the proof goes through $\Lambda$.
