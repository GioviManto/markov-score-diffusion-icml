\section{What survives diffusion noise: attenuation by statistical order}
\label{sec:theory}

Write $\gamma$ for the standard Gaussian on $\Reals^{\nsites}$, $\Hen{k}$ for the
probabilists' Hermite polynomials and $H_\nu(\vx)=\prod_i\Hen{\nu_i}(x_i)$ for their
products, of degree $|\nu|$, with $\E_\gamma[H_\nu H_\mu]=\nu!\,\delta_{\nu\mu}$. The
channel \cref{eq:channel} acts on Hermite moments diagonally.

\begin{theorem}[Hermite attenuation]
\label{thm:hermite}
For every multi-index $\nu$ with $\E_{\Pz}|\va|^{|\nu|}<\infty$,
$\;\E_{\Pt}[H_\nu(\vx_t)]=e^{-|\nu|t}\,\E_{\Pz}[H_\nu(\va)]$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Mehler's identity $\E_{z}[\Hen{n}(\alpha a+\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\,z)]=\alpha^n\Hen{n}(a)$ for
$z\sim\normal{0}{1}$ follows from the generating function
$e^{sx-s^2/2}=\sum_n\Hen{n}(x)s^n/n!$; coordinates are independent given $\va$, and
$\alpha=e^{-t}$.
\end{proof}
Nothing here requires $\Pz/\gamma\in L^2(\gamma)$---which fails for the Laplace chain and,
because $\lmax(\Sig_0)>2$, for the Gaussian chain at $\corr=0.85$---only finite moments.
Two consequences are exact at every $t$: cumulants of order $m\ge3$ scale as $e^{-mt}$
(Gaussian noise has none), and the degree-4 Hermite moments of two variance-matched laws
differ exactly by their fourth-cumulant tensors $\kfour$, attenuated by $e^{-4t}$.
\emph{Three clocks.} For a feature first visible at Hermite degree $m$, its
\emph{amplitude} decays as $e^{-mt}$ (exact, \cref{thm:hermite}); the \emph{Fisher
information} a sequence carries about it, a squared amplitude, as $e^{-2mt}$
(\cref{thm:decay,thm:general}); and the \emph{number of sequences} needed for a fixed
local precision grows as $e^{2mt}$ (\cref{cor:samples}). Correlation lives at degree two:
$e^{-2t}$, $e^{-4t}$, $e^{4t}$. Non-Gaussian shape first appears at degree four:
$e^{-4t}$, $e^{-8t}$, $e^{8t}$. \emph{Diffusion noise is a low-pass filter on
statistical order.}

The consequence for scores and for learning is an expansion in the signal-to-noise
ratio $e^{-2t}$. Let $s=e^{-t}\vx/\Dt$, $b=e^{-2t}/\Dt$ and
$\Lambda(s,b)=\log\E_{\Pz}\exp(s\cdot\va-b|\va|^2/2)$, so that
$\log\Pt(\vx)=\log\gamma_{\Dt}(\vx)+\Lambda(s,b)$ and
$\score=-\vx/\Dt+(e^{-t}/\Dt)\nabla_s\Lambda$ exactly. Grading $\Lambda$ by order in
$e^{-t}$, its second-order part is the same for $\Pz$ and its covariance twin, and its
fourth-order part differs by a term linear in $\kfour$. The expansion is legitimate when
$\Lambda$ is finite in a neighbourhood of the origin, which is the one hypothesis the
theorem needs:
\begin{assumption}[Exponential moment]
\label{ass:exp}
$\E_{\Pz}e^{\lambda|\va|}<\infty$ for some $\lambda>0$.
\end{assumption}
Every innovation law used below satisfies it except Student-$t$, for which
$\Lambda(s,0)=+\infty$ at every $s\neq0$; that family is discussed after
\cref{thm:general}.

\begin{theorem}[Order-by-order information decay]
\label{thm:decay}
Let $\Pz$ satisfy \cref{ass:exp} with covariance $\Sig_0$, let $\Sgauss$ be the score
of its covariance twin, and fix $\nsites$. As $t\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
  \E_{\Pt}\lVert\score-\Sgauss\rVert^2
   &= \tfrac{1}{6}\Frob{\kfour}^2\,e^{-8t}(1+\epsilon_t),
  \label{eq:scoregap}\\
  I_t(\theta)&= \tfrac12\Frob{\partial_\theta\Sig_0}^2\,e^{-4t}(1+\epsilon_t)
  \ \text{if }\partial_\theta\Sig_0\neq0,
  \label{eq:info2}\\
  I_t(\theta)&= \tfrac1{24}\Frob{\partial_\theta\kfour}^2\,e^{-8t}(1+\epsilon_t)
  \ \text{if }\partial_\theta\Sig_0=0,
  \label{eq:info4}
\end{align}
the last with $\partial_\theta\kfour\neq0$, and with $\epsilon_t=O(e^{-2t})$,
where $I_t(\theta)=\E_{\Pt}[(\partial_\theta\log\Pt)^2]$ is the Fisher information one
noised sequence carries about a parameter $\theta$ of $\Pz$, and $\Frob{\cdot}$ is the
Frobenius norm of the matrix or symmetric 4-tensor over ordered indices. For a Gaussian
chain \cref{eq:info2} is exact at every $t$ with
$I_t=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Sig_t^{-1}\partial_\theta\Sig_t\Sig_t^{-1}\partial_\theta\Sig_t)$
and $\epsilon_t=O(e^{-2t}\lmax(\Sig_0))$ explicitly.
\end{theorem}
The information statements \cref{eq:info2,eq:info4} are the diffusion-time form of a
principle established for low-SNR latent-variable models: the log-likelihood expands in
powers of the SNR with the order-$k$ term carrying $k$-th-moment information
\citep{katsevich2023likelihood,perry2019sample,bandeira2023estimation}, and Fisher
information about directions first identifiable at moment order $k$ scales as
$\mathrm{SNR}^k$ \citep{balanov2026gmm}; with $\mathrm{SNR}_t\simeq e^{-2t}$ these are
\cref{eq:info2,eq:info4}. The cumulant attenuation behind \cref{thm:hermite} is also
written down for the forward process by \citet{aarts2025cumulants}. What this paper
adds is the score-level statement \cref{eq:scoregap} with its constant, made
interpretable by \cref{prop:lmmse}; the general order and its constant below; the
hypothesis under which the expansion is a theorem and what happens outside it; and the
exact verification and learning consequences that follow. The proof
(\cref{app:proofs}) expands $\Lambda$ to fourth order on the bulk $\{|s|\le\lambda/2\}$,
where \cref{ass:exp} makes it analytic with uniformly bounded tilted moments, takes the
$\gamma$-second moment of the leading Hermite term by Wick's theorem, and bounds the
tail, whose probability is $\exp(-c\,e^{2t})$ under \cref{ass:exp}; the scalar leading
term of \cref{eq:scoregap} is $(\kfour/6)\Hen{3}(x)e^{-4t}$. The leading orders of a
degree-2 and a degree-4 coordinate do not couple, because $\Hen{2}$ and $\Hen{4}$ are
$\gamma$-orthogonal, so the \emph{efficient} information of a shape coordinate after
lower-degree nuisance coordinates are estimated has the same rate and constant as its
raw information; a nuisance of the same degree costs an $O(1)$ factor.

\begin{theorem}[General order]
\label{thm:general}
Let $\Pz^\theta$ satisfy \cref{ass:exp} uniformly near $\theta_0$, and let $m$ be the
smallest degree at which $\partial_\theta\E_{\Pz}[H_\nu(\va)]\neq0$ for some $|\nu|=m$.
Then, at fixed $\nsites$,
\[
  I_t(\theta)=e^{-2mt}\sum_{|\nu|=m}\frac{\bigl(\partial_\theta\E_{\Pz}[H_\nu(\va)]\bigr)^2}{\nu!}
  \,\bigl(1+O(e^{-2t})\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}
The constant is the Hermite norm of the derivative of the degree-$m$ moment tensor; for
$m=2$ it is $\tfrac12\Frob{\partial_\theta\Sig_0}^2$ and for $m=4$ at fixed covariance
$\tfrac1{24}\Frob{\partial_\theta\kfour}^2$, since the multiset $\nu$ collects $4!/\nu!$
ordered index tuples. A direction with no non-vanishing Hermite footprint is not
identifiable at any noise level; under \cref{ass:exp} the law is determined by its
moments, so such a direction is null.

\begin{corollary}[Sample complexity by order]
\label{cor:samples}
Fix $t$ and let $\theta$ be a coordinate of lowest degree $m$ with efficient information
$I_t^{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta)$. For $\nseq$ i.i.d.\ sequences observed at level $t$ and any
estimator sequence, the local asymptotic minimax risk satisfies
$\liminf_{\nseq}\sup_{|\theta'-\theta|\le c/\sqrt{\nseq}}\nseq\,\E_{\theta'}(\hat\theta-\theta')^2\ge1/I_t^{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta)=\Theta(e^{2mt})$,
and maximum likelihood attains it. A covariance coordinate therefore needs
$\nseq\propto e^{4t}$ sequences for a fixed local precision, a shape coordinate
$\nseq\propto e^{8t}$; the ratio of the two informations is $\propto e^{-4t}$.
\end{corollary}
This is H\'ajek--Le Cam for a regular parametric model at fixed $t$; it says nothing
uniform in $t$ (the limits do not commute), nothing about absolute precision for a bounded
coordinate once $1/\sqrt{\nseq I_t}$ exceeds its range, and nothing about an optimiser
at a finite budget. All three caveats are visible in \cref{sec:learning}.

\paragraph{Where the asymptote begins.}
For the Gaussian chain the expansion parameter is $e^{-2t}\lmax(\Sig_0)$, with
$\lmax\le(1+\corr)/(1-\corr)$, and $\tstar=\tfrac12\log\lmax(\Sig_0)$ is an exact scale:
$\Pt/\gamma\in L^2(\gamma)$ iff $e^{2t}>\lmax-1$, and below $\tstar$ the long-wavelength
covariance modes are still of order one and the rates are shallower. For non-Gaussian
laws the same scale is a heuristic that the measurements below support. At $\corr=0.85$,
$\tstar=\gaussTstareightfive$: a strongly correlated chain keeps its correlation
information longer, and any measurement stopped near $\tstar$ sees slopes short of
$-4$ and $-8$.

\paragraph{Heavy tails.}
Student-$t_\nu$ innovations violate \cref{ass:exp}. For a regularly varying tail of index
$\nu$ the expected squared gap acquires a term of order $e^{-\nu t}$ carried by
sequences with $|\va|\gtrsim e^{2t}$, whose probability is itself of order $e^{-\nu t}$:
in expectation the Hermite law holds while $\tfrac16\Frob{\kfour}^2e^{-8t}$ dominates it,
and on any sample of practical size the Hermite law is all that is seen
(\cref{app:numerics}). We state this as an observation supported by quadrature, not as a
theorem; the moment condition of \cref{thm:decay} is not merely technical, and
\cref{fig:theory}c shows Student-$t_5$ reaching the constant on a domain wide enough to
hold its samples.

\paragraph{Verification with the exact score.}
\Cref{fig:theory} tests \cref{thm:decay} where nothing has to be approximated. For the
Gaussian chain the closed form gives slope $\gaussSlopeRhoeightfive$ above $\tstar$ at
$\corr=0.85$ and the scaled information $\gaussScaledRhoeightfivetone$, $\gaussScaledRhoeightfivettwo$,
$\gaussScaledRhoeightfivetthree$ at $t=1,2,3$. For the non-Gaussian chain the shape information,
estimated from per-sequence scores obtained by Fisher's identity through the tree-exact
recursion, falls with slope $\shapeSlopeBetatwo$ ($\beta=2$) and $\shapeSlopeBetathree$
($\beta=3$) above $\tstar$, against the predicted $-8$, while the correlation
information falls with slope $\shapeSlopeRhotwo$ against $-4$; the kinked Laplace density
($\beta=1$) reaches the quadrature floor of the outer-product estimate near $t=2.5$. The
constants are verified at two levels of precision: in the scalar case an untruncated
quadrature reproduces the shape constant of \cref{eq:info4} to within
$\audScalarConstWorst\%$ for $\beta\in\{\audScalarBetaList\}$ at
$t\ge\audScalarFromLevel$ and the covariance constant of \cref{eq:info2} to five digits
(\cref{app:audit}); on the chain the outer-product estimate reaches the shape constant
only to within twenty per cent before its floor. The closure gap on the Laplace chain
decays with slope $\closureSlopeSqlaplace$ and reproduces the constant
$\tfrac16\Frob{\kfour}^2$ ($\closureCfourlaplace$ for $\nsites=32$) to
$\audConstRatioLaplaceTfour$ $[\audConstRatioLoLaplaceTfour,\audConstRatioHiLaplaceTfour]$
at $t=4$ from $\audConstChains$ sequences; the per-sequence gap is heavy-tailed
(maximum $\audConstMaxOverMedianLaplaceTfour$ times the median), which is why so many are
needed. The theorem also explains a measurement the previous literature on this model
reported as a discrepancy: information slopes of about $-3$ and $-6$ obtained over
$t\le1.6$ are the pre-asymptotic values below $\tstar=\gaussTstareightfive$, not a failure
of the $e^{-4t}$, $e^{-8t}$ law.

\paragraph{The gap is the fourth-order term.}
\Cref{eq:scoregap} says what the covariance twin lacks to leading order: the degree-4
Hermite term carried by $\kfour$. Adding it back exactly---the first Edgeworth correction
around the twin, with the true $\Sig_0$ and $\kfour$ (\cref{app:audit})---closes
$\audEdgeClosedLaplaceTzeropsixeight$ of the squared gap at
$t=\audEdgeLevelTzeropsixeight$, $\audEdgeClosedLaplaceTonepfourthree$ at
$t=\audEdgeLevelTonepfourthree$ and all of it from $t=2$ on the Laplace chain, and the
same on the bimodal and uniform chains; below $t=\audEdgeDivergesBelowLaplace$ the
expansion diverges and the correction hurts. This is the moment-method reading of the
theorem: above the crossover a score built from the first four moments is the score.

\paragraph{What this says about score learning.}
An estimator that carries only second-order structure cannot go below the LMMSE floor
$\lVert\score-\Sgauss\rVert$, which on the Laplace chain at $\corr=0.85$ is
$\lmmseFloorLaplace$ of the score energy pooled over the twelve-level schedule and
$\lmmseLowestLaplace$ at the lowest level (\cref{app:oracles}). A learner that recovers the
fourth-order structure has, by \cref{cor:samples}, $e^{4t}$ fewer effective samples per
sequence at level $t$ than one that recovers only covariance: the twelve-level schedule
is, for shape, effectively a four-level one. Both facts organise the experiments of
\cref{sec:experiments}.
