  \caption{\textbf{Capacity buys exactly what the closure discards.} Pooled relative score
  error against the innovation's excess kurtosis for the generalised-Gaussian family at
  fixed $\corr$ and variance ($\nseq=\famSize$, $\famSeeds$ seeds, $\pm$ s.e.): the
  Gaussian-innovation kernel tracks the LMMSE floor, which is the exact price of
  second-order closure for each member; the mixture kernel tracks the exact score, and

Moving the innovation from Laplace (excess kurtosis $+3$) through the Gaussian to the
platykurtic end at fixed $\corr$ and variance (\cref{fig:family}), the LMMSE floor traces a
V: $\famLmmseBone$ at the Laplace end, exactly zero at $\beta=2$ where the covariance twin
\emph{is} the truth, and $\famLmmseBsix$ at the platykurtic end. The Gaussian-innovation
kernel follows that V within its own standard error at every member, which is
\cref{prop:lmmse} read across a family rather than at a point; the mixture kernel stays
between $\famEmeightBtwo$ and $\famEmeightBone$ throughout. The paired gain of $\ncomp=8$
over $\ncomp=1$ is therefore ordered by $\kfour$: $\famDiffBone$
($[\famDiffBonelo,\famDiffBonehi]$) at Laplace, $\famDiffBoneptwofive$ at
$\kappa_4=\famKurtValBoneptwofive$, and $\famDiffBsix$ at the platykurtic end. At the
Gaussian member the gain reverses: $\famDiffBtwo$
($[\famDiffBtwolo,\famDiffBtwohi]$), a small but unambiguous \emph{loss}, because there is
no fourth-order structure to buy and the extra components only add estimation variance.
Non-Gaussian capacity is worth what \cref{thm:decay} says it is worth---the fourth-order
structure the covariance twin leaves behind---and nothing where the twin leaves nothing.
