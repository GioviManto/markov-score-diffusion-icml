\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Generative diffusion produces samples by reversing a noising process, and the reversal
is driven by the score $\score(\vx,t)=\nabla_{\vx}\log\Pt(\vx)$ of the noised data law
\citep{sohl2015deep,ho2020denoising,song2021scorebased}. Training regresses a network onto that score through
denoising \citep{vincent2011connection}, and every question about what such a network has learned
is a question about the distance between what it computes and the score it should have
computed. For realistic data the target is unavailable, so a large error cannot be
separated from a hard target; the tractable alternatives are Gaussian, whose scores are
linear, or mixtures, whose nonlinearity lives in the mixture index rather than in any
dependence between coordinates.

We study a family that is neither. A first-order Markov chain with continuous,
non-Gaussian transitions has a globally dependent, nonlinear score, yet under
coordinatewise Ornstein--Uhlenbeck noise its posterior factor graph is a tree, so the
score is computable exactly by two sweeps of functional message passing
(\cref{sec:setup}). Because the chain's covariance is fixed by two numbers and its
higher-order structure by the innovation law, the model separates second-order from
higher-order structure by construction: Gaussian, Laplace, Student-$t$, uniform and
bimodal innovations, and a one-parameter family that interpolates between them, share
one covariance exactly.

\paragraph{The question.}
What information about structured data survives diffusion noise, and how much does
knowing the structure reduce the data needed to recover the score and the dynamics that
generate it?

\paragraph{Diffusion noise is a low-pass filter on statistical order.}
The first answer is a theorem (\cref{sec:theory}). Under the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck channel
the Hermite moment of degree $m$ is attenuated exactly by $e^{-mt}$; for a law with an
exponential moment the Fisher information a noised sequence carries about a feature first
visible at degree $m$ decays as $e^{-2mt}$, with a constant we give in general.
Correlation lives at degree two and its information decays as $e^{-4t}$; non-Gaussian
shape first appears at degree four and decays as $e^{-8t}$; and the gap between the
true score and the score of the covariance-matched Gaussian---which we prove is exactly
what single-Gaussian message closure computes (\cref{prop:lmmse})---is
$\tfrac16\Frob{\kfour}^2e^{-8t}$ to leading order, with a crossover
$\tstar=\tfrac12\log\lmax(\Sig_0)$ that is exact for the Gaussian chain. The exact
score lets us verify the exponents and the constants (\cref{fig:theory}), show that the
missing fourth-order term is the whole of the gap above the crossover, and explain,
rather than contradict, earlier measurements on this model that stopped before $\tstar$.

\paragraph{Learning through the channel.}
The second answer is empirical. We learn the transition law from noised sequences
alone, by expectation--maximisation around the same exact recursion, with Fisher's
identity supplying the exact gradient of the marginal likelihood from one
forward--backward pass (\cref{sec:learning}). The information curve predicts the level at
which the shape of the innovation law stops being identifiable from a given number of
sequences; the fitted shape sits at the information reference below that level and
collapses to a Gaussian innovation at it, while the correlation survives to the
crossover. Against denoising networks trained on the clean trajectories---a fully
connected network, a weight-shared window head, and dilated, recurrent, attention-based
and multiscale architectures under one training recipe---the structured estimator is
ahead at every sample size (\cref{sec:experiments}); at the estimator's own information
interface, one noised view per sequence with neither a clean chain nor the true score
for model selection, the best network is a factor $\matchedRatioBestD$ behind. Given
enough clean data the networks do cross the second-order floor of \cref{prop:lmmse}, so
the gap is a statement about the information interface and not about a barrier they
cannot pass. We show that this advantage is earned by the non-Gaussian capacity of the
innovation model and not only by the Markov factorisation, that it transfers to the
generated distribution, that it extrapolates in sequence length, and that it ends at a
misspecification strength that a closed-form asymptotic bias predicts.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i)~An exactly solvable non-Gaussian, correlated diffusion laboratory with a validated
score reference, and the identification of Gaussian message closure with the LMMSE of
the covariance twin (\cref{prop:lmmse}), used as an apparatus check at every regime.
(ii)~\Cref{thm:hermite,thm:decay,thm:general}: exact Hermite attenuation and the
order-by-order decay of score gaps and Fisher information, with constants, the general
order, the exponential-moment hypothesis and what lies beyond it, verified with the
exact score.
(iii)~Transition learning from noised data by exact-inference EM, placed against the
information curve level by level, with the collapse of the shape coordinate located and
a moment estimator as the rate-correct, inefficient competitor.
(iv)~A controlled comparison against learned denoisers at three declared information
interfaces---capacity attribution, fair architecture screen, matched training with no
oracle, generation, length extrapolation and misspecification with its closed-form
bias---showing what explicit structure buys and where it stops.
