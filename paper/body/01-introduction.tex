\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A diffusion model generates by reversing a noising process, and the reversal is driven by
the score $\score(\vx,t)=\nabla_{\vx}\log\Pt(\vx)$ of the noised data law
\citep{sohl2015deep,ho2020denoising,song2021scorebased}. Training regresses a network onto
that score through denoising \citep{vincent2011connection}, so every question about what the
network has learned is a question about the distance between what it computes and the score
it should have computed. For real data that target is unknown: a large error cannot be told
from a hard target, and the solvable alternatives are Gaussian, whose scores are linear, or
mixtures, whose nonlinearity lives in a discrete label rather than in any dependence between
coordinates.

\paragraph{A laboratory with an exact, non-trivial score.}
We study a family that is neither. A first-order Markov chain with continuous, non-Gaussian
transitions has a globally dependent, nonlinear score, yet under coordinatewise
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck noise its posterior factor graph is a tree, so two sweeps of functional
message passing return the score exactly (\cref{sec:setup}). The chain's covariance is fixed
by two numbers and everything beyond it by the driving-noise law, so Gaussian, Laplace,
Student-$t$, uniform and bimodal driving noise, and a one-parameter family interpolating
between them, share one covariance exactly and differ only in higher-order structure. The
laboratory therefore separates, by construction, what diffusion noise does to correlation
from what it does to shape.

\paragraph{Three questions.}
What information about structured data survives diffusion noise? Can the dynamics that
generate the data be learned from noised sequences alone, and how efficiently? And how much
does knowing the structure reduce the data needed to recover the score, compared with a
network that must discover it?

\paragraph{Three answers.}
The first is a theorem (\cref{sec:theory}): \emph{diffusion noise is a low-pass filter on
statistical order}. The channel attenuates the Hermite moment of degree $m$ exactly by
$e^{-mt}$; for a law with an exponential moment, the Fisher information a noised sequence
carries about a feature first visible at degree $m$ decays as $e^{-2mt}$, with a constant
we give in general, and the number of sequences needed for a fixed local precision grows as
$e^{2mt}$. Correlation lives at degree two and non-Gaussian shape at degree four, so the
shape is lost twice as fast. The gap between the true score and that of the
covariance-matched Gaussian, which we prove is exactly what single-Gaussian message closure
computes (\cref{prop:lmmse}), is $\tfrac16\Frob{\kfour}^2e^{-8t}$ to leading order, with a
crossover $\tstar=\tfrac12\log\lmax(\Sig_0)$ that is exact for the Gaussian chain. The exact
score lets us verify the exponents and the constants, show that the missing fourth-order
term is the whole of the gap above the crossover, and explain, rather than contradict,
earlier measurements on this model that stopped before $\tstar$.

The second is empirical (\cref{sec:learning}). We learn the transition law from noised
sequences alone by expectation--maximisation around the same exact recursion, with Fisher's
identity supplying the exact likelihood gradient from one forward--backward pass. The
information curve of \cref{sec:theory} predicts the level at which the shape of the
driving-noise law stops being identifiable from a given number of sequences, and the fits do
what it says: they sit at the information reference below that level and collapse to
Gaussian driving noise at it, while the correlation survives to the crossover.

The third is a controlled comparison (\cref{sec:experiments}). Against six sequence
denoisers trained under one recipe the structured estimator is ahead at every sample size;
at the estimator's own information interface, one noised view per sequence with neither a
clean chain nor the true score for model selection, the best network is a factor
$\matchedRatioBestD$ behind, a larger gap than at the standard interface. The advantage is
earned by the non-Gaussian capacity of the driving-noise model and not by the Markov
factorisation alone, transfers to generation, extrapolates in sequence length, and ends at a
misspecification strength that a closed-form bias predicts. Given enough clean data the
networks do pass the second-order floor of \cref{prop:lmmse}, so the gap is a statement
about information, not about a barrier they cannot cross.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i)~An exactly solvable non-Gaussian, correlated diffusion laboratory with a validated score
reference, and the identification of Gaussian message closure with the LMMSE of the
covariance twin (\cref{prop:lmmse}), used as an apparatus check at every regime.
(ii)~\Cref{thm:hermite,thm:decay,thm:general}: exact Hermite attenuation and the
order-by-order decay of score gaps and Fisher information, with constants, the general
order, the exponential-moment hypothesis and what lies beyond it, verified with the exact
score. (iii)~Transition learning from noised data by exact-inference EM, placed against the
information curve level by level, with the collapse of the shape coordinate located and a
moment estimator as the rate-correct, inefficient competitor. (iv)~A comparison against
learned denoisers at three declared information interfaces, from capacity attribution to
misspecification with its closed-form bias, showing what explicit structure buys and where
it stops.
