\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The model is a chain in one dimension with a linear autoregressive form; the theorem
holds for any law with the stated moments, but the exact verification and the learning
results are for chains. The estimator's advantage is the correctness of its
factorisation: under a non-Markov truth it is misspecified in a way more data does not
repair, and \cref{sec:experiments} locates where that begins. Inference costs
$O(\nsites\ngrid^2)$ per sequence, far above a network forward pass, so the advantage is
in data and understanding, not in wall-clock. The comparison with denoisers equalises
the number of clean sequences under each method's standard recipe and, separately,
matches the information interface; it does not rank learning algorithms in general.

What the laboratory teaches is general: diffusion noise erases statistical structure
order by order, at rates fixed by Hermite degree, so a score learner that reaches only
second order is bounded away from the truth by an $e^{-8t}$-scaled term and a learner
of shape pays $e^{4t}$ in samples per level. Explicit structure is the way to recover
what the data still carry; the price of the wrong structure is a bias that no sample
size removes.
