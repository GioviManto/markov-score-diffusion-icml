\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The model is a chain in one dimension with a linear autoregressive form; the theorems
hold for any law with an exponential moment at fixed length, and heavy-tailed laws sit
outside them with a regime of their own; the exact verification and the learning
results are for chains, at one innovation family in most experiments and with eight
seeds in the regime sweeps. The estimator's advantage is the correctness of its
factorisation: under a non-Markov truth it is misspecified in a way more data does not
repair, and \cref{sec:experiments} computes where that begins. Inference costs
$O(\nsites\ngrid^2)$ per sequence and level---of order ten times a network forward pass at
$\ngrid=401$, with the mixture M-step of the same order per EM iteration---so the advantage
is in data and understanding, not in wall-clock: a factor of eight in error at equal data
is bought with a structural assumption and with compute a network does not pay. The comparison with denoisers equalises the number of clean sequences under each
method's standard recipe and, separately, matches the information interface with no
oracle on either side; it does not rank learning algorithms in general.

What the laboratory teaches is general: diffusion noise erases statistical structure
order by order, at rates fixed by Hermite degree, so a score learner that reaches only
second order is bounded away from the truth by an $e^{-8t}$-scaled term whose content is
the fourth-order moment, and a learner of shape pays $e^{4t}$ in samples per level.
Explicit structure is the way to recover what the data still carry; the price of the
wrong structure is a bias that no sample size removes, and that bias can be computed.
