\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Solvable diffusion and the Gaussian part of the score.}
Diffusion and score matching are due to \citet{sohl2015deep,ho2020denoising,song2021scorebased}
and \citet{hyvarinen2005estimation,vincent2011connection}; the score--posterior-mean identity is
Tweedie's \citep{robbins1956empirical,efron2011tweedie}. Exactly solvable targets have been
Gaussian \citep{pierret2024gaussian}, mean-field or mixture-like
\citep{biroli2023generative,biroli2024dynamical,cui2025solvable}, or hierarchical with discrete
leaves \citep{sclocchi2025phase}, on which U-Nets and transformers implement belief propagation
\citep{mei2024unets,mei2023deep,garnierbrun2024transformers}. A recurrent
empirical observation is that learned scores are dominated by their Gaussian part over a wide
noise range \citep{wang2024gaussian,li2024hidden,kadkhodaie2024generalization,wang2026rmt}, and that
downstream statistics depend on the first two moments alone \citep{ghane2025universality}. Our chain is the degenerate tree of depth $\nsites$ with
continuous non-Gaussian states; the Gaussian part of its score is the Kalman smoother of the
covariance twin (\cref{prop:lmmse}), and \cref{eq:scoregap} is the sharp rate, with its constant and
crossover, behind the dominance those papers report.

\paragraph{What survives noise: the low-SNR literature.}
\Cref{thm:decay} belongs to the low-SNR expansions of estimation theory. In Gaussian channels the
mutual information and the MMSE expand in the SNR with fourth-order statistics entering at second
order, for arbitrary input laws \citep{prelov2004second,guo2005mutual,dytso2023meta,kong2023information}; for latent-variable
models the log-likelihood expands with the order-$k$ term matching $k$-th moments
\citep{katsevich2023likelihood}, which yields the $\sigma^{2k}$ sample complexity of multireference
alignment and orbit recovery \citep{perry2019sample,bandeira2023estimation} and, most closely, the
layered Fisher-information geometry of \citet{balanov2026gmm}, where information about directions
first identifiable at moment order $k$ scales as $\mathrm{SNR}^k$ and the method of moments is
almost efficient. Under the OU channel $\mathrm{SNR}_t\simeq e^{-2t}$, so that statement and our
$e^{-2mt}$ are the same law in different clocks. \citet{aarts2025cumulants} write the
forward-process cumulant attenuation $e^{-nt}$ and show that score networks regenerate the
cumulants. The information law is therefore not new here; what this paper adds is the score-level
statement with its constant $\tfrac16\|\kappa_4\|_F^2$ and its content (the fourth-order term
closes the gap), the general-order constant, the hypothesis under which the expansion is a theorem
and the heavy-tailed regime outside it, the crossover $\tstar$ as an exact scale, the verification
of rates and constants against an exact non-Gaussian dependent score, and the learning consequences
with an exact E-step. Speciation and collapse times \citep{biroli2024dynamical,achilli2026speciation}
and the information-restricted Bayesian analysis of \citet{hunt2026exact} are second-moment phenomena
in this vocabulary; heavy-tailed forward processes \citep{pandey2024heavy,tang2026tweedie} change the
channel and therefore the ladder.

\paragraph{Learning from noisy data only.}
Ambient diffusion learns a clean distribution from corrupted samples by predicting a further
corruption \citep{daras2023ambient,daras2024consistent}; EM with diffusion posterior sampling in
the E-step \citep{rozet2024learning,bai2024em} and SURE-type surrogates \citep{kawar2024gsure}
address the same problem nonparametrically. Our learner is parametric and its E-step is exact,
so the question changes from ``can the clean law be recovered'' to ``how fast is it lost'', which
\cref{thm:decay} answers order by order. The estimator is classical: sum-product on a chain is
forward--backward \citep{baum1970,rabiner1989tutorial}, grid quadrature is Kitagawa's smoother
\citep{kitagawa1987}, Fisher's identity and EM are \citet{dempster1977maximum,cappe2005}, and the
Gaussian special case is the Shumway--Stoffer algorithm \citep{shumway1982em}. Consistency of the
maximum-likelihood estimator for general-state hidden Markov models is \citet{douc2011consistency};
the identifiability of a Markov signal observed through independent noise, which is what makes
the one-view protocol of \cref{sec:learning} well posed, is \citet{gassiat2020identifiability}.
Spectral and moment methods for hidden Markov models \citep{hsu2012spectral} are the natural
high-noise competitor; the moment estimators of \cref{sec:learning} are their simplest form here,
rate-correct and inefficient below the crossover.

\paragraph{Physical Markov models and the field benchmark.}
A nearest-neighbour energy on a line is the one-dimensional Gibbs measure of statistical mechanics, solved
exactly by a transfer matrix \citep{ising1925beitrag}, which is also why it has no phase transition at any positive
temperature; its Markov structure on a lattice is the Hammersley--Clifford picture of \citet{besag1974spatial}.
Our $\phi^4$ benchmark is a one-dimensional instance, chosen so that the exact score, the exact samples and the
exact physical statistics are available from one eigensolve and every estimator can be judged against them and
against a physical reading (temperature, correlation length, kink density). The same section of the literature
that studies when generative diffusion commits to a mode, speciation \citep{biroli2024dynamical,achilli2026speciation},
is the vocabulary of \cref{sec:field}'s commitment measurement, which we make exact on a chain; and the cumulant
attenuation of the forward process \citep{aarts2025cumulants} is the same law as \cref{thm:hermite}.
