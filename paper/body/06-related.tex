\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Solvable diffusion and the Gaussian part of the score.}
Diffusion and score matching are due to \citet{sohl2015deep,ho2020denoising,song2021scorebased}
and \citet{hyvarinen2005estimation,vincent2011connection}; the score--posterior-mean identity is
Tweedie's \citep{robbins1956empirical,efron2011tweedie}. Exactly solvable targets have been
Gaussian \citep{pierret2024gaussian}, mean-field or mixture-like
\citep{biroli2023generative,biroli2024dynamical,raya2023spontaneous,cui2025solvable,ghio2024sampling},
or hierarchical with discrete leaves \citep{sclocchi2025phase,sclocchi2025probing}, on which
U-Nets and transformers implement belief propagation
\citep{mei2024unets,mei2023deep,garnierbrun2024transformers,favero2025compositional}. A recurrent
empirical observation is that learned scores are dominated by their Gaussian part over a wide
noise range \citep{wang2024gaussian,li2024hidden,kadkhodaie2024generalization,wang2026rmt}, and
that downstream statistics of generated data depend on the first two moments alone
\citep{ghane2025universality}. Our chain is the degenerate tree of depth $\nsites$ with
continuous non-Gaussian states; the Gaussian part of its score is the Kalman smoother of the
covariance twin (\cref{prop:lmmse}), and \cref{eq:scoregap} is the sharp rate, with constant and
crossover, behind the dominance those papers report. Kamb and Ganguli's local-score machines
\citep{kamb2024analytic} are the analogous closed form for convolutional image models; here the
closed form is exact.

\paragraph{What survives noise: the low-SNR literature.}
\Cref{thm:decay} belongs to the low-SNR expansions of estimation theory. In Gaussian channels the
mutual information and the MMSE expand in the SNR with fourth-order statistics entering at second
order \citep{prelov2004second,guo2005mutual,dytso2023meta,kong2023information}; for latent-variable
models the log-likelihood expands with the order-$k$ term matching $k$-th moments
\citep{katsevich2023likelihood}, which yields the $\sigma^{2k}$ sample complexity of multireference
alignment and orbit recovery \citep{perry2019sample,bandeira2023estimation} and, most closely, the
layered Fisher-information geometry of \citet{balanov2026gmm}, where information about directions
first identifiable at moment order $k$ scales as $\mathrm{SNR}^k$. Under the OU channel
$\mathrm{SNR}_t\simeq e^{-2t}$, so that statement and our $e^{-2mt}$ are the same law in different
clocks. \citet{aarts2025cumulants} write the forward-process cumulant attenuation $e^{-nt}$ and
show that score networks regenerate the cumulants. What is not in that literature, and is what
this paper adds, is the score-level statement for a Markov (dependent) rather than i.i.d.\ latent
structure, its constant $\tfrac16\|\kappa_4\|_F^2$ and crossover $\tstar$, the exact verification
on a chain, and the learning consequences with an exact E-step. Speciation and collapse times
\citep{biroli2024dynamical,achilli2026speciation} and the information-restricted Bayesian analysis
of \citet{hunt2026exact} are second-moment phenomena in this vocabulary; heavy-tailed forward
processes \citep{pandey2024heavy,tang2026tweedie} change the channel and therefore the ladder.

\paragraph{Learning from noisy data only.}
Ambient diffusion learns a clean distribution from corrupted samples by predicting a further
corruption \citep{daras2023ambient,daras2024consistent}; EM with diffusion posterior sampling in
the E-step \citep{rozet2024learning,bai2024em} and SURE-type surrogates \citep{kawar2024gsure}
address the same problem nonparametrically. Our learner is parametric and its E-step is exact,
so the question changes from ``can the clean law be recovered'' to ``how fast is it lost'', which
\cref{thm:decay} answers order by order. The estimator is classical: sum-product on a chain is
forward--backward \citep{baum1970,rabiner1989tutorial}, grid quadrature is Kitagawa's smoother
\citep{kitagawa1987}, Fisher's identity and EM are \citet{dempster1977maximum,cappe2005}, and the
Gaussian special case is the Shumway--Stoffer algorithm \citep{shumway1982em}. Consistency of the
maximum-likelihood estimator for general-state hidden Markov models is \citet{douc2011consistency};
the identifiability of a Markov signal observed through independent noise, which is what makes
the one-view protocol of \cref{sec:learning} well posed, is \citet{gassiat2020identifiability}.
Spectral and moment methods for hidden Markov models \citep{hsu2012spectral} are the natural
high-noise competitor that \cref{cor:samples} suggests.

\paragraph{Sequence denoisers and length.}
The baselines of \cref{sec:experiments} follow the standard sequence architectures
\citep{bai2018tcn,vaswani2017attention,ronneberger2015unet}; length extrapolation of
attention-based models depends on the positional encoding
\citep{press2022alibi,kazemnejad2023positional}, and sequence diffusion models that generalise
past the training horizon do so by construction \citep{chen2024diffusionforcing}. Here the
reference at every length is the exact score, so length generalisation is measured rather than
assumed.
