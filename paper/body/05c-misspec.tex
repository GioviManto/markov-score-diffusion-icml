\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_misspec.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Where the structure stops helping.} Ratio of the window head's error
  to EM--BP's against the misspecification strength, for four Gaussian departures from
  the chain with exact reference scores (rank-one global latent, long-range precision
  coupling, an AR(2) second coefficient at fixed lag-one correlation, site-dependent
  $\corr$), at two sample sizes. Above the dotted line the misspecified chain estimator
  still wins.}
  \label{fig:misspec}
\end{figure}
Every departure is Gaussian so that its score is exact, and every arm is scored against
that score. A rank-one global latent is absorbed by the chain's fitted $\corr$ and never
reverses the ordering (ratio $\misRatioGlobalEoneD$ at $\beta=1$); long-range
precision coupling reverses it between $\gamma=\misBreakLoLongrangeD$ and
$\misBreakHiLongrangeD$ at $\nseq=2048$ (interpolated crossing
$\misBreakLongrangeD$) and between $\misBreakLoLongrangeC$ and $\misBreakHiLongrangeC$
at $\nseq=512$; an AR(2) second coefficient reverses it at
$\misBreakArtwoD$. The two sizes separate the statistical gain of the structure at
small $\nseq$ from its asymptotic bias at large $\nseq$: where the ratio falls with
$\nseq$, the chain estimator has converged to a wrong law and more data no longer helps.
