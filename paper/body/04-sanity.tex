\section{A sanity check: the Gaussian case}
\label{sec:sanity}

\paragraph{The covariance twin and the price of second-order closure.}
The Gaussian chain with the same $\corr$ and $\ivar$ as \cref{eq:linear} has the closed-form score
$\Sgauss(\vx,t)=-\Sig_t^{-1}\vx$, $\Sig_t=e^{-2t}\Sig_0+\Dt\Id$, and posterior mean
$e^{-t}\Sig_0\Sig_t^{-1}\vx$, the linear minimum-mean-square (LMMSE) estimator. It is the natural
approximation of a non-Gaussian chain by something solvable, and BP shows exactly what it computes.
\begin{proposition}[Gaussian closure computes the LMMSE of the covariance twin]
\label{prop:lmmse}
Let $\Pgauss$ project a density onto the Gaussian with its mean and variance, and let
moment-projected sum-product apply $\Pgauss$ to each message after every transition
integral in \cref{eq:bp}. For the chain \cref{eq:linear} with $\corr\neq0$ and any
driving-noise law of mean zero and variance $\ivar$, it returns exactly the LMMSE estimator
$e^{-t}\Sig_0\Sig_t^{-1}\vx$, the same for every such law.
\end{proposition}
The proof (\cref{app:proofs}) is an induction over the two sweeps: a convolution's first two
moments depend on the driving noise only through its first two moments, so every projected message
equals the Gaussian chain's message. Two consequences are used as checks below. Sum-product with
\emph{any} Gaussian kernel is a linear map of $\vx$, and the LMMSE of the true covariance minimises
the posterior-mean error over linear maps, so the LMMSE floor bounds every linear estimator; and the
Gaussian quasi-likelihood of noised data depends on the data only through their sample covariance,
whose expectation is $e^{-2t}\Sig_0+\Dt\Id$, so a linear-Gaussian kernel fitted by maximum likelihood is
consistent for $(\corr,\ivar)$ and lands on that floor. \Cref{prop:lmmse} thus turns the gap between
$\score$ and $\Sgauss$ into an interpretable object: it is precisely what any estimator that carries only
second-order structure---a linear-Gaussian kernel, a linear denoiser, or the Kalman smoother---must
leave on the table.

\paragraph{BP with Gaussian messages is the Kalman smoother.}
On a Gaussian chain everything is available in closed form, which makes it the one place where grid
BP can be checked against two independent exact computations.
\begin{proposition}[The Gaussian chain: score, smoother and sum-product coincide]
\label{prop:kalman}
Let the driving noise of \cref{eq:linear} be Gaussian, so that $\va\sim\normal{0}{\Sig_0}$ with
$(\Sig_0)_{ij}=\corr^{|i-j|}$, and let $\vx$ follow \cref{eq:channel}. Then
(i) $\score(\vx,t)=-\Sig_t^{-1}\vx$ and $\pmean{\va}=e^{-t}\Sig_0\Sig_t^{-1}\vx$, with
$\Sig_t=e^{-2t}\Sig_0+\Dt\Id$, and Tweedie's identity holds between them; (ii) every message of
\cref{eq:bp} is Gaussian, $\lmsg_i$ is the Kalman filter's prediction density
$p(a_i\mid x_{1:i-1})$ and $\lmsg_i\like$ its filtering density, and the site marginals
$\propto\lmsg_i\like\rmsg_i$ are those of the Rauch--Tung--Striebel smoother, whose means are
therefore $\pmean{\va}$ of (i).
\end{proposition}
The proof is in \cref{app:proofs}. The forward sweep is the Kalman filter and the full pass is its
smoother, so a Gaussian BP is a linear map of the data and has little use beyond serving as a reference;
this is what makes it a sanity check for any approximation of BP, grid BP included. \Cref{fig:gaussian}
measures it. A Rauch--Tung--Striebel smoother written without the BP code agrees with the closed form to
$\gsRtsWorst$, and grid BP at the operating grid agrees with both to $\gsOpWorst$ at every one of the
$\gsLevels$ noise levels. Refining the grid converges faster than any power of the spacing, as the trapezoid
rule does on Gaussian integrands, and reaches $10^{-10}$ by $\ngrid=\gsNeedTmin$ at the smallest level,
where the likelihood is narrowest, and by $\ngrid=\gsNeedTmax$ at the largest; widening the domain at the
operating spacing removes the truncation error, which is $\gsAthreeTmin$ at $\halfwidth=3$.

\paragraph{What does not carry over.}
Moment-projected BP run \emph{on the grid} is off by up to $\gsProjWorst$ even on the Gaussian chain: its
backward messages are Gaussians whose variance grows like $e^{2t}$, and the grid truncates them at
$\pm\halfwidth$; the proposition concerns functional messages, and the LMMSE floors reported here are
computed in closed form. On the $\phi^4$ field the projected recursion is not well posed at all, because
the backward messages are nearly flat (a fraction $\fldProjEdgeBase$ of their mass lies in the outer
$5\%$ of the grid on the base cell and $\fldProjEdgeOthersMin$ or more on the others), so the Gaussian
arm of every field comparison is the covariance twin itself, computed in closed form
(\cref{sec:field}). We also do not call the Gaussian pass a Kalman \emph{filter}: it smooths.
