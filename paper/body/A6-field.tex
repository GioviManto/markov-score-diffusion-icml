\section{The $\phi^4$ field in detail}
\label{app:field}

\paragraph{The chain.}
With the site terms split symmetrically, the lattice energy of \cref{sec:setup} defines the symmetric, positive,
Hilbert--Schmidt transfer operator $\transfer(\phi,\phi')=e^{-\latt V(\phi)/2\Temp}e^{-(\phi'-\phi)^2/2\Temp\latt}e^{-\latt V(\phi')/2\Temp}$.
Its largest eigenvalue $\lambda_0$ is simple with a positive eigenfunction $\psi_0$ (Perron--Frobenius), and the
infinite-volume Gibbs measure is the stationary chain with marginal $\pi=\psi_0^2$ and kernel
$\kernel(\phi'\mid\phi)=\transfer(\phi,\phi')\psi_0(\phi')/(\lambda_0\psi_0(\phi))$, reversible with respect to $\pi$. The lag-$k$
covariance is $\sum_n(\lambda_n/\lambda_0)^k\langle\psi_0|\phi|\psi_n\rangle^2$, so $\lambda_1/\lambda_0$ plays for the field the role
$\corr$ plays for the linear chain, and $\corrlen=1/\log(\lambda_0/\lambda_1)$. We diagonalise $\transfer$ densely on $2401$ points on
$[-12,12]$; samples are exact in the continuum (a rejection sampler on the tilted walk) and on the lattice (an inverse-CDF sampler), and
the two agree.

\paragraph{The tilted-walk identity and reversibility.}
Rearranging the eigen-equation, $\kernel(\phi'\mid\phi)=\normal{\phi';\phi}{\Temp\latt}g(\phi')/Z(\phi)$ with $g=\psi_0e^{-\latt V/2\Temp}$ and
$Z(\phi)=\lambda_0\psi_0(\phi)e^{\latt V(\phi)/2\Temp}$: a Gaussian step tilted by a function of the arrival point, with the column normaliser the
$h$-transform. Every tilted walk $\kernel_g(a'\mid a)=\normal{a';a}{\bondvar}g(a')/Z_g(a)$ is reversible with invariant density $\pi_g\propto gZ_g$, since
$\pi_g(a)\kernel_g(a'\mid a)\propto g(a)g(a')\normal{a';a}{\bondvar}$ is symmetric. It is the Gibbs chain of the symmetric pair energy
$W(a,a')=(a'-a)^2/2\bondvar+f(a)+f(a')$ with $f=-\tfrac12\log(g/Z_g)$, and conversely; so the directed kernel and the symmetric pair energy are one model
class. For the field's own $(\bondvar,g)$, $f=(\latt/2\Temp)V(sa)$ up to a constant, $\hat\Temp=\hat\bondvar s^2/\latt$ reads the temperature, and
$\hat V(\phi)=(2\hat\Temp/\latt)\hat f(\phi/s)+\mathrm{const}$ reads the potential, where $s^2=\operatorname{Var}_\pi\phi$.

\paragraph{Where the linear chain and the field meet, and why the mixture kernel fails.}
A Gaussian linear chain with $0<\corr<1$ is the member of the tilted family with $\log g$ quadratic (step variance $\ivar/\corr$, tilt variance $1+\corr$);
no other linear chain is, and no other tilted walk has state-independent driving noise, so the two families intersect at the Gaussian chain only. The
mixture kernel is misspecified on the field through its \emph{drift}: the conditional mean step of a tilted walk is exactly $\bondvar(\log Z)'(a)$, the
gradient of the log-normaliser, which for the field is $\Temp\latt(\log\psi_0)'(\phi)+(\latt^2/2)V'(\phi)$, a nonlinear function with its zeros inside the wells,
while the mixture's drift is linear. The misspecification is therefore through the drift, not through the shape of the driving noise, which is a different
mechanism from every Gaussian departure of \cref{fig:misspec}.

\paragraph{Physical statistics.}
A \emph{kink} is a sign change that survives merging every run shorter than one kink width $\sqrt2/\latt$ sites; a \emph{domain} is the run between two kinks;
the bimodality coefficient is $(\text{skew}^2+1)/(\text{excess kurtosis}+3)$. The raw sign-change density per unit length grows like $\latt^{-1/2}$ under
refinement ($\fldRawKinkA$, $\fldRawKinkB$, $\fldRawKinkC$ at $\latt=1,\tfrac12,\tfrac14$) and is not a physical quantity; the coarse density is constant
across the three refinements to within $\fldCoarseKinkSpread$ and the exact samples reproduce the spectral correlation function to within
$\fldCorrZMax$ standard errors on every cell. \Cref{tab:fieldsamples} compares generated and exact samples.

\paragraph{The chain outside the family.}
The Laplace-bond chain of \cref{sec:kernelclass} has the symmetric pair weight $e^{W(a,a')}$ (pair energy $-W$) with $W(a,a')=-|a'-a|/\lambda-\eta\,[V(a)+V(a')]/2$ and $V(a)=(a^2-1)^2/4$, bond scale $\lambda=0.40$ and well depth $\eta=2.0$,
chosen before any fit so that its lag-one correlation and kurtosis match the base cell's; it is a reversible grid chain built from this symmetric $W$ by the same Perron--Frobenius construction as the field's, so exact samples and an exact oracle are available from the grid.
It lies outside the tilted-walk class: a Laplace bond is not a Gaussian step times a function of the arrival point.

\paragraph{Missing data and uncertainty.}
Hiding a random half or a gap of $8$ or $16$ of the $32$ sites of a noised sequence ($t=0.5$), and drawing $200$ posterior sequences by forward-filter
backward-sample on the grid with the kernels of \cref{sec:field} \emph{without refitting}, the learned site energy and spline are indistinguishable from the
oracle: error ratio $\fldInpaintOracleErrMin$--$\fldInpaintOracleErrMax$, interval-width ratio $\fldInpaintOracleWidthMin$--$\fldInpaintOracleWidthMax$,
coverage $\fldInpaintCovMin$--$\fldInpaintCovMax$ of a nominal $90\%$ interval (the oracle's own is $\fldInpaintOracleCov$: the grid and the quantile estimate cost about $\fldInpaintGridCost$). The
mixture kernel behaves like the Gaussian smoother. The Gaussian smoother with the true covariance is $\fldInpaintLinErrMin$--$\fldInpaintLinErrMax$ worse
in error on the base cell, with $\fldInpaintLinWidthMin$--$\fldInpaintLinWidthMax$ wider intervals, and within $1\%$ in error at $\Temp=1$: at this noise level
the exact posterior buys little over a Gaussian smoother.

\paragraph{Clean data.}
Fitting on the clean sequences instead of the noised ones helps the spline and the MDN by about a factor two but not the mixture, whose
risk is $\fldCleanMixRatioMin$--$\fldCleanMixRatioMax$ of its noised risk: its gap is its wrong drift, not the noise. The one-view
channel costs the spline a factor of about two to two and a half in risk at every $\nseq$.

\paragraph{Commitment.}
For a noised sequence the posterior mean of the commitment of site $i$ is $\E|2P(a_i>0\mid\vx)-1|$. For the Gaussian twin the posterior of $a_i$ is
$\normal{\mu_i}{v_i}$ with $\mu_i\sim\normal{0}{\Sigma_{ii}-v_i}$ over the data, so with $R_i^2=1-v_i/\Sigma_{ii}$ the commitment is
$\tfrac2\pi\arcsin R_i$ in closed form. The twin is half-committed exactly where its posterior variance has halved. The registered readings, their verdicts
and the discussion of the fit of the dilute-kink law (the local slope of $\ln\corrlen_{\mathrm{phys}}$ against $1/\Temp$ rises from $\fldKinkSlopeHot$ to
$\fldKinkSlopeCold$ as $\Temp$ falls from $\fldKinkTHigh$ to $\fldKinkTLow$, against the instanton value $2\sqrt2/3-\Temp/2$ with a gap shrinking roughly like $\Temp^2$) are in the
experiment registry (\textsc{e50}).

\paragraph{Training.}
EM's E-step is linear in the number of sequences and its M-step does not depend on it, so training time is dominated by the E-step beyond a few hundred sequences.
In shared-node runs, a network fit took $\fldTrainNetMin$ to $\fldTrainNetMax$ minutes at $\nseq=2048$ and an EM fit $\fldTrainEmMin$ minutes to $\fldTrainEmMax$ hours (indicative:
the runs shared nodes), so training is not where the structured estimator wins.
