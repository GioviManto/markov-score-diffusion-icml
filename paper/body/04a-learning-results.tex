\Cref{fig:learning} measures both with $\nseq=\learnSize$ Laplace-chain sequences at
$\corr=\learnRho$ and $\learnSeeds$ replicates per level, against the information
reference read from the exact-score computation of \cref{sec:theory}: the Cram\'er--Rao
bound $1/\sqrt{\nseq\,I_t}$ for the correlation and, through the chain rule at the
Laplace member of the one-parameter shape family, for the excess kurtosis. The
estimator sits at the bound: its error is $\learnEffRhoMean\times$ the bound for
$\widehat\corr$ and $\learnEffKurtMean\times$ for $\widehat\kfour$, averaged over the
levels at which either is informative. What the channel does is therefore read off the
bound itself. The shape bound grows as $e^{4t}$ and crosses the full range of the
coordinate ($3$) at $t=\learnKurtBoundCross$; at $t=1$ the fitted excess kurtosis is
$\learnRmseKurtTone$ from the truth, the fit having collapsed to a Gaussian innovation,
while $\widehat\corr$ is still within $\learnRmseRhoTone$. Correlation is lost only past
the crossover $\tstar=\learnTstar$: $\learnRmseRhoTonepfour$ at $t=\learnLevelTonepfour$ and
$\learnRmseRhoTtwo$ at $t=2$, where the estimator also leaves the bound. That last
departure we report as unresolved rather than as a finding: at $t\ge\learnAtCapHighFrom$
the validation-selected checkpoint is the last rung of the \textsc{em} ladder in
$\learnAtCapHighMin$ of cells or more, against at most $\learnAtCapLowMax$ at every
informative level, so at those two levels the fit is limited by the iteration budget and
the excess over the bound cannot be attributed to the channel. A control run with the
ladder extended sixfold is registered (\textsc{e06c}); nothing else in this paragraph
depends on it. Below the
crossover both errors grow more slowly than the asymptotic rates, as
\cref{sec:theory} predicts (fitted log-slopes $\learnPreSlopeRho$ and
$\learnPreSlopeKurt$ on $[\learnPreLo,\learnPreHi]$ against $2$ and $4$); the asymptotic
window itself lies beyond the reach of $\nseq=\learnSize$ sequences at this
correlation, because the shape estimate saturates before $\tstar$. At $t=0.1$ the
transition law is recovered to Hellinger distance $\learnHellTzeropone$, and the
clean-data fit gives the optimiser's own floor, $\learnRmseRhoTzero$ and
$\learnRmseKurtTzero$.
