\section{Setup: an exactly solvable non-Gaussian laboratory}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data and channel.}
A clean sequence $\va\in\Reals^{\nsites}$ is a first-order Markov chain,
\begin{equation}
  \Pz(\va)=\mu(a_1)\prod_{i=2}^{\nsites}\kernel(a_i\mid a_{i-1}),\qquad
  \kernel(a'\mid a)=\innov(a'\!-\!\corr a),
  \label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
with $a_1\sim\normal{0}{1}$, $|\corr|<1$, and an innovation density $\innov$ of mean zero
and variance $\ivar=1-\corr^2$. The variance choice makes $\Var(a_i)=1$ and
$\Cov(a_i,a_j)=\corr^{|i-j|}$ exactly for \emph{every} innovation law, so the families we
use---Gaussian, Laplace, Student-$t$, uniform, a symmetric two-component mixture, and the
generalised Gaussian $\innov_\beta(\varepsilon)\propto\exp(-|\varepsilon/s_\beta|^\beta)$
whose exponent $\beta$ moves the excess kurtosis from $+3$ (Laplace) through $0$
(Gaussian) to $-1.1$ at fixed variance---share one covariance $\Sig_0$ and differ only
through cumulants of order four and higher.
The forward process is the coordinatewise Ornstein--Uhlenbeck channel
\begin{equation}
  \vx_t = e^{-t}\va + \sqrt{\Dt}\,\vz,\qquad \Dt=1-e^{-2t},\quad \vz\sim\normal{0}{\Id},
  \label{eq:channel}
\end{equation}
so $\Var(x_{t,i})=1$ at every $t$ and the noised law $\Pt$ tends to $\normal{0}{\Id}$.

\paragraph{The score is a posterior mean, and the posterior is a tree.}
Tweedie's identity gives $\score(\vx,t)=-(\vx-e^{-t}\pmean{\va})/\Dt$, with
$\pmean{\va}=\E[\va\mid\vx_t=\vx]$ \citep{robbins1956empirical,efron2011tweedie}. Conditioning
\cref{eq:prior} on $\vx$ multiplies the prior by one unary factor
$\like(x_i\mid a_i)\propto\exp[-(x_i-e^{-t}a_i)^2/2\Dt]$ per site: no edge is added among
the latent variables, the posterior factor graph is the prior's chain, and sum-product
on it is exact \citep{pearl1988probabilistic,kschischang2001factor}. Two message sweeps,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \lmsg_i(a)&\propto\!\int\!\kernel(a\mid a')\,\like(x_{i-1}\mid a')\,\lmsg_{i-1}(a')\dd a',\\
  \rmsg_i(a)&\propto\!\int\!\kernel(a''\mid a)\,\like(x_{i+1}\mid a'')\,\rmsg_{i+1}(a'')\dd a'',
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bp}
\end{equation}
return the site marginals $\propto\lmsg_i\like\rmsg_i$ and hence the exact score of a
correlated, non-Gaussian, $\nsites$-dimensional law. Messages are functions on $\Reals$;
we represent them on a grid of $\ngrid$ points on $[-\halfwidth,\halfwidth]$ with
trapezoidal quadrature, which makes the recursion exact for the discretised factor model
and controlled for the continuous one (\cref{app:numerics}: at $\ngrid=401$,
$\halfwidth=8$ the reference is accurate to $10^{-5}$ relative on ordinary draws for
every family used). Throughout, ``the score'' means this tree-exact functional inference,
evaluated by validated grid quadrature.

\paragraph{The covariance twin and the price of second-order closure.}
The Gaussian chain with the same $\corr$ and $\ivar$ has the closed-form score
$\Sgauss(\vx,t)=-\Sig_t^{-1}\vx$, $\Sig_t=e^{-2t}\Sig_0+\Dt\Id$, and posterior mean
$e^{-t}\Sig_0\Sig_t^{-1}\vx$, the linear minimum-mean-square (LMMSE) estimator.
\begin{proposition}[Gaussian closure computes the LMMSE of the covariance twin]
\label{prop:lmmse}
Let $\Pgauss$ project a density onto the Gaussian with its mean and variance, and let
moment-projected sum-product apply $\Pgauss$ to each message after every transition
integral in \cref{eq:bp}. For the chain \cref{eq:prior} with $\corr\neq0$ and any
innovation law of mean zero and variance $\ivar$, it returns exactly the LMMSE estimator
$e^{-t}\Sig_0\Sig_t^{-1}\vx$, the same for every such law.
\end{proposition}
The proof (\cref{app:proofs}) is an induction over the two sweeps: a convolution's first
two moments depend on the innovation only through its first two moments, so every
projected message equals the Gaussian chain's message. \Cref{prop:lmmse} turns the gap
between $\score$ and $\Sgauss$ into an interpretable object: it is precisely what any
estimator that carries only second-order structure---a Gaussian innovation kernel, a
linear denoiser, or the Kalman smoother---must leave on the table. \Cref{sec:theory}
computes its size.
