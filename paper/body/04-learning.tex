\section{Learning the dynamics through noise}
\label{sec:learning}

Now drop the assumption that $\kernel$ is known. We observe $\nseq$ noised sequences
$\vx^{\mu}$, each at one level $t_\mu$, never the clean chain, and maximise the marginal
log-likelihood $\Lik(\params)=\sum_\mu\log\Pt[t_\mu,\params](\vx^\mu)$ of a
parameterised kernel $\kernelth$.

\paragraph{The exact gradient from one sweep.}
Because only the transition factors depend on $\params$, Fisher's identity
\citep{cappe2005} gives, on the grid,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \nabla_\params\Lik(\params)&=\bigl\langle\Xis(\params),\,\nabla_\params\log\kernelth\bigr\rangle,\\
  \Xis_{kl}&=\sum_{\mu}\sum_{i\ge2}P(a_i=\gridpt{k},a_{i-1}=\gridpt{l}\mid\vx^\mu),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
where the expected transition mass $\Xis$ is read off the pairwise marginals that the
same forward--backward pass returns. No differentiation through the recursion is needed,
and the identity is verified against finite differences and against brute-force
enumeration of the evidence on short chains (\codefile{tests/test\_em\_bp.py},
\codefile{tests/test\_fisher\_bruteforce.py}). $\Xis$ is also all the M-step of EM needs: for a Gaussian kernel
the maximiser is the belief-weighted Yule--Walker pair
$\corr=S_{01}/S_{00}$, $\ivar=(S_{11}-\corr S_{01})/W$ with
$S_{00}=\sum_{kl}\Xis_{kl}\gridpt{l}^2$, $S_{01}=\sum_{kl}\Xis_{kl}\gridpt{k}\gridpt{l}$,
$S_{11}=\sum_{kl}\Xis_{kl}\gridpt{k}^2$ and $W=\sum_{kl}\Xis_{kl}$ ($k$ indexes the child
site, $l$ the parent); for the mixture kernel
$\kernelth(a'\mid a)=\sum_{c=1}^{\ncomp}\pi_c\,\normal{a'-\corr a-\nu_c}{s_c^2}$ used below,
the complete-data problem carries a second latent label and we take conditional-maximisation
sweeps, a generalised EM whose ascent is monotone \citep{dempster1977maximum,meng1993maximum}. The
estimator sees one frozen noised view per sequence and never a clean trajectory; each
reported fit selects its iteration count on a validation bundle disjoint from the test
bundle.

\paragraph{The information curve predicts what is learned at each level.}
\Cref{cor:samples} makes a prediction about learning that the exact score does not
need: fitting from $\nseq$ sequences all observed at level $t$, the shape of the
driving-noise law stops being identifiable at the level where its Cram\'er--Rao reference
crosses the coordinate's range, while the correlation survives to the crossover.
